## Part 1 of 7 — Purpose, Scope, Governance, Definitions, and Contacts

## 1. About This Policy

### 1.1 Title and Identification

This is the **Patient Rights, Privacy (HIPAA), Telehealth & Portal Use Policy** of Lakeside Regional Medical Center (LRMC), policy document **PRIV-400**. It defines patients' rights and responsibilities; how LRMC protects health information under HIPAA and grants proxy access; how patients enroll in and use the **MyLRMC** patient portal; the rules for telehealth (eligibility, consent, and technology); and how patients raise complaints and grievances, including the role of the **Patient Advocate**.

LRMC is a 400-bed community teaching hospital and outpatient network at 1200 Lakeshore Parkway, Rochester, NY 14600. The hospital main line is 585-230-0000. The **Patient Advocate / Office of Patient Experience is reachable at 585-230-4000**; the **Privacy Officer** is at 585-230-4010. The MyLRMC Portal Help Desk is 585-230-3000, and LRMC Virtual Care (telehealth) is 585-230-3500.

### 1.2 Why This Policy Exists

Patients trust LRMC with their health and their most private information, and increasingly interact with LRMC through digital tools. This policy exists to:

1. **Honor patient rights.** State clearly what patients can expect — respectful care, information, choice, privacy, and a voice — and what responsibilities support their care.
2. **Protect privacy.** Apply HIPAA and state law to safeguard health information, and define how trusted others (proxies) may be granted access.
3. **Enable safe digital access.** Set rules for enrolling in and using MyLRMC and for telehealth, so these tools are convenient, accessible, and safe.
4. **Give patients a voice.** Provide an accessible complaints and grievance process and a Patient Advocate to help resolve concerns.

### 1.3 How to Use This Policy

Each numbered section stands on its own. If you need only the proxy-access rules, the portal-enrollment steps, the telehealth-consent requirements, or how to file a grievance, you should be able to act from that single section. This policy is referenced throughout the LRMC policy library as the authority on privacy, identity/proxy verification, the portal, and telehealth.

### 1.4 A Safety Note

This policy governs **rights, privacy, access, and technology**, not clinical triage. When a message about these topics also contains clinical content — a symptom or health concern — the clinical content is handled first under **PCT-100** and **EUA-150** (a possible emergency is directed to 911/ED). The portal and telehealth are **not** for emergencies; this is stated to patients at enrollment and on the message and visit screens.

## 2. Scope and Applicability

### 2.1 What This Policy Covers

This policy applies across LRMC and its affiliated network to: patient rights and responsibilities; protection, use, and disclosure of protected health information under HIPAA and state law; identity verification and proxy/authorized-representative access; MyLRMC portal enrollment, use, and security; telehealth eligibility, consent, and technology; and the complaints/grievance process and Patient Advocate function.

### 2.2 What This Policy Does Not Cover

This policy does **not** govern clinical care or triage (PCT-100, EUA-150); the operational detail of the legal medical record (HIM-300); the mechanics of billing and records-release fees (FIN-700, which applies this policy's privacy rules); or insurer requirements. It is the **authority on privacy, proxy, portal, and telehealth** that the other policies reference.

### 2.3 Who Must Follow This Policy

This policy applies to **all LRMC workforce members** who access, use, or disclose patient information or who support patients' rights, portal access, or telehealth — clinical and non-clinical staff, the portal and telehealth support teams, the Privacy Officer, and the Patient Advocate. Every workforce member is responsible for protecting patient privacy and honoring patient rights.

## 3. Guiding Principles

### 3.1 Dignity and Respect

Every patient is treated with dignity and respect, free from discrimination, regardless of age, race, ethnicity, national origin, language, religion, disability, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, or ability to pay.

### 3.2 Privacy as a Default

Health information is private by default. Staff access and disclose only the minimum necessary, only for permitted purposes, and only to verified, authorized recipients. Privacy protections are stronger for sensitive categories.

### 3.3 Patient Choice and Voice

Patients have a say in their care and in how LRMC communicates with them, and they have accessible ways to raise concerns without fear of affecting their care.

### 3.4 Equitable Access

Digital tools (portal, telehealth) are offered with attention to language, disability, and the digital divide, and non-digital alternatives (phone, in person) are always available so no patient is excluded.

### 3.5 Safety Boundary

The portal and telehealth are convenient but not continuously monitored and not for emergencies; patients are consistently directed to 911 or the nearest emergency department for emergencies.

## 4. Governance

This policy is owned by the **Privacy, Rights & Patient Experience Committee (PRPEC)**, chaired by the Privacy Officer (585-230-4010) with the Patient Advocate (585-230-4000) and representation from compliance, health information management, clinical leadership, portal/telehealth operations, and language/accessibility services. It is reviewed at least **annually** and when law or regulation changes. Privacy concerns and potential breaches are reported to the Privacy Officer (585-230-4010) and Compliance / Risk Management (585-230-8100).

## 5. Definitions and Contacts

**Protected health information (PHI).** Individually identifiable health information that HIPAA protects, in any form. LRMC limits its use and disclosure to permitted purposes and the minimum necessary.

**HIPAA.** The federal law setting national standards for protecting health information, including patients' rights to access and to control certain disclosures of their information.

**Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP).** The document describing how LRMC may use and disclose PHI and a patient's privacy rights, provided to patients and available on request.

**Treatment, payment, and health care operations (TPO).** The core permitted purposes for which PHI may be used and disclosed without specific authorization.

**Authorization.** A signed, HIPAA-compliant permission required for uses/disclosures beyond TPO and for sensitive information; revocable by the patient.

**Proxy / authorized representative.** A person authorized to access a patient's information and act on their behalf (for example, a parent of a minor, a legal guardian, or a personal representative), verified before access is granted.

**MyLRMC portal.** LRMC's secure patient portal and mobile app for messaging, results, scheduling, records, and bill pay. Not for emergencies.

**Telehealth / virtual care.** A clinical visit conducted by video or phone with an LRMC clinician (LRMC Virtual Care, 585-230-3500). Not for emergencies.

**Grievance.** A formal complaint by a patient (or representative) about care or services that requires investigation and a written response, as distinguished from a concern resolved promptly at the point of service.

**Patient Advocate.** The staff role that helps patients understand their rights, navigate concerns, and resolve complaints and grievances (585-230-4000).

## Part 2 of 7 — Patient Rights and Responsibilities

## 6. Patient Rights

Every patient at Lakeside Regional Medical Center has the following rights. Staff honor these rights and help patients exercise them.

### 6.1 Respectful, Nondiscriminatory Care

Patients have the right to considerate, respectful care that is free from discrimination and from abuse, neglect, or harassment, and that respects their cultural, religious, and personal values.

### 6.2 Information and Communication

Patients have the right to understandable information about their condition, treatment, and expected outcomes; to know the names and roles of those caring for them; and to communication aids — including interpreters (585-230-6400) and accommodations for disabilities (585-230-6450) — at no cost to them.

### 6.3 Participation and Informed Consent

Patients have the right to participate in decisions about their care, to give **informed consent** or refuse treatment to the extent permitted by law, to be informed of the risks and benefits and alternatives, and to formulate **advance directives** and have them honored.

### 6.4 Privacy and Confidentiality

Patients have the right to personal privacy during care and to confidentiality of their health information, protected under HIPAA and Part 3 of this policy.

### 6.5 Access to Records

Patients have the right to access and obtain copies of their medical records, and to request amendments, as described in Part 3 and FIN-700 (Release of Information, 585-230-3600).

### 6.6 Choice of Communication

Patients have the right to choose how LRMC communicates with them (for example, by portal, phone, or mail) and to request confidential communications — for example, contact at a specific number or address — which LRMC accommodates where reasonable.

### 6.7 Freedom From Unnecessary Restraint

Patients have the right to be free from restraint or seclusion except as clinically necessary and permitted by policy and law.

### 6.8 Pain Management and Comfort

Patients have the right to appropriate assessment and management of pain and to considerate care that supports comfort and dignity.

### 6.9 A Voice and Freedom From Retaliation

Patients have the right to voice concerns, file complaints and grievances (Part 6), and recommend changes **without fear that it will affect their care**. Raising a concern never results in retaliation.

### 6.10 Knowledge of Rights and Costs

Patients have the right to be informed of these rights, to receive the Notice of Privacy Practices, and to information about the cost of their care and financial-assistance options (FIN-700).

## 7. Patient Responsibilities

To support safe, effective care, patients are asked to:

- **Provide accurate information** about their health history, medications, and coverage, and update it when it changes.
- **Participate** in their care, ask questions when something is unclear, and tell their care team if they cannot follow a plan.
- **Keep or cancel appointments** in advance when possible (SRA-250), and use the portal and telehealth as intended — not for emergencies.
- **Treat others with respect** — staff and other patients — and help maintain a safe environment.
- **Meet financial responsibilities** to the extent able, and reach out about payment plans or assistance if cost is a barrier (FIN-700).
- **Protect their own access** — keep portal credentials private and report suspected misuse (Part 4).

## Part 3 of 7 — HIPAA, Privacy, and Proxy Access

## 8. How LRMC Protects Health Information

### 8.1 Permitted Uses and the Minimum-Necessary Rule

LRMC uses and discloses protected health information (PHI) for **treatment, payment, and health care operations (TPO)** as HIPAA permits, and for other purposes only with the patient's **authorization** or as required/permitted by law. For uses other than treatment, staff apply the **minimum-necessary** standard — accessing and sharing only what is needed for the task. Looking at a record without a work-related need is prohibited and is a privacy violation.

### 8.2 The Notice of Privacy Practices

LRMC provides every patient a **Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP)** describing how their information may be used and disclosed and their privacy rights. The NPP is available at registration, in MyLRMC, and on request, and in accessible formats and languages (585-230-6400, 585-230-6450).

### 8.3 Patient Privacy Rights Under HIPAA

Patients have the right to: access and obtain copies of their records (FIN-700; Release of Information, 585-230-3600); request an **amendment** of information they believe is inaccurate; request an **accounting of certain disclosures**; request **restrictions** on certain uses/disclosures; request **confidential communications** (such as contact at an alternate address); and receive notice of a breach affecting their information. Staff explain how to exercise each right and the timeframe to respond.

### 8.4 Sensitive Information

Certain categories — mental-health, substance-use, HIV, reproductive-health, and similar information — receive heightened protection under state and federal law and are disclosed only with the appropriate, specific authorization. Staff apply these protections in messaging, records release, and proxy access (BH-600 for behavioral-health).

### 8.5 Identity Verification

Before discussing PHI or acting on a request, staff verify they are communicating with the patient or a verified authorized party. Verification methods are proportional to the channel and the sensitivity of the information. Unverified requests do not receive PHI.

### 8.6 Safeguards, Breaches, and Reporting

LRMC maintains administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for PHI. Suspected privacy violations or breaches — including misdirected messages, lost devices, or improper access — are reported immediately to the **Privacy Officer (585-230-4010)** and Compliance / Risk Management (585-230-8100), investigated, and handled (including patient notification) as required by law. Reporting is expected and protected.

## 9. Proxy and Authorized-Representative Access

### 9.1 What a Proxy Is

A **proxy** (authorized representative) is a person authorized to access a patient's health information and act on their behalf — for example, a parent of a minor, a legal guardian, a personal representative, or an adult the patient designates to help manage their care. Proxy access is granted only after authority is **verified**.

### 9.2 Granting Proxy Access

To establish proxy access, the patient (or legally authorized person) completes the proxy-authorization process through the MyLRMC Help Desk (585-230-3000) or in person, specifying who is authorized and the scope. Staff verify identity and the legal basis for the authority before enabling access. The patient may **revoke** proxy access at any time.

### 9.3 Scope and Limits of Proxy Access

Proxy access can be full or limited, and is governed by the patient's wishes and by law. Special rules apply to:

- **Minors:** access for parents/guardians follows state law, which may limit a parent's access to certain adolescent-confidential services (such as some reproductive- or behavioral-health care). Staff apply these rules so that protected adolescent information is handled correctly.
- **Adults who designate a proxy:** access is limited to the scope the patient authorizes.
- **Patients who lack capacity:** access follows the legal personal-representative rules.

### 9.4 Sensitive Information and Proxies

Even an authorized proxy may not automatically receive sensitive categories (Section 8.4); these follow the specific legal protections and the patient's authorization. Staff confirm the scope before releasing sensitive information to a proxy.

### 9.5 Verifying a Proxy in Messaging and Records

In any portal message, records request, or billing/records interaction, staff confirm that a person acting "on behalf of" a patient is a **verified** proxy before sharing PHI (this rule is referenced across PCT-100, FIN-700, and SRA-250). Unverified third-party requests are not honored.

## Part 4 of 7 — MyLRMC Portal Enrollment and Use

## 10. About the MyLRMC Portal

The **MyLRMC** portal and mobile app are LRMC's secure way for patients to message their care team, view results and records, schedule and manage appointments, request refills, and pay bills. The portal is convenient but is **not continuously monitored and not for emergencies** — for an emergency, patients call 911; for an urgent after-hours clinical concern, they call the after-hours nurse line (585-230-0911). The MyLRMC Help Desk (585-230-3000) supports enrollment and technical issues.

## 11. Enrollment

### 11.1 Who Can Enroll

Established LRMC patients age 18 and older may enroll, and authorized proxies may enroll for minors and dependent adults under the proxy rules in Part 3. Enrollment requires identity verification.

### 11.2 How to Enroll

Patients enroll by accepting an invitation provided at registration or a visit, by self-enrolling with identity verification online, or with help from the MyLRMC Help Desk (585-230-3000) or front-desk staff. During enrollment, patients set up secure credentials and are encouraged to enable additional login security (such as two-step verification).

### 11.3 Accessibility and Alternatives

Enrollment and the portal are offered with language support (585-230-6400) and accessibility features (585-230-6450). Patients who cannot or prefer not to use the portal are fully served by phone (Central Scheduling 585-230-2600; Help Desk 585-230-3000) and in person — the portal is never the only way to access care or information.

## 12. Using the Portal

### 12.1 What the Portal Is For

The portal is for non-urgent messaging, viewing results and records, scheduling, refills, and bill pay. Messages are reviewed during business hours and handled under PCT-100's response-time standards. Patients are reminded at the point of composing a message that the portal is not for emergencies.

### 12.2 Results Release in the Portal

Results may be released to the portal under HIM-300 and PCT-100: normal/reassuring results may post with explanation, while some sensitive or complex results are communicated by the clinician directly. Patients can ask questions about any result through the portal, and clinical questions are answered within scope (PCT-100).

### 12.3 Security and Patient Responsibilities

Patients are responsible for keeping their credentials private, logging in only on trusted devices, and reporting any suspected unauthorized access to the MyLRMC Help Desk (585-230-3000) and, for privacy concerns, the Privacy Officer (585-230-4010). LRMC protects the portal with security safeguards and monitors for misuse.

### 12.4 Proxy Use and Teen Privacy in the Portal

Proxy access in the portal follows Part 3, including the special rules for minors and adolescent-confidential information. The portal is configured so that protected adolescent information is handled according to law, and proxies see only what they are authorized to see.

### 12.5 Acceptable Use

The portal is used for the patient's own care and communication. Abusive, threatening, or fraudulent use may result in restrictions, handled with notice and with the Patient Advocate's involvement (585-230-4000); clinical access to care is preserved through other channels even if portal use is restricted.

### 12.6 Technical Support

For login problems, app issues, or enrollment help, patients contact the MyLRMC Help Desk (585-230-3000). Technical failures affecting the portal are escalated to IT while patient care continues through phone and in-person channels.

## Part 5 of 7 — Telehealth Eligibility, Consent, and Technology Requirements

## 13. About Telehealth at LRMC

**Telehealth** (LRMC Virtual Care, 585-230-3500) lets patients have a clinical visit by video or phone with an LRMC clinician. It expands access and convenience for many needs but is **not for emergencies** and not suitable when a physical exam or in-person testing is required. A telehealth encounter that reveals a red flag is redirected to emergency or in-person care per EUA-150.

## 14. Eligibility

### 14.1 When Telehealth Is Appropriate

Telehealth is appropriate for needs that can be safely managed remotely — for example, many follow-ups, medication management, behavioral-health visits without a crisis, review of stable results, and some minor acute issues (SRA-250, Part 2). Suitability is confirmed when the visit is scheduled; if a visit turns out to need an exam or testing, it is converted to in-person care.

### 14.2 When Telehealth Is Not Appropriate

Telehealth is not appropriate for emergencies (call 911), for problems requiring hands-on examination, procedures, or in-person testing, or for behavioral-health crises requiring immediate safety response (BH-600; 911/585-230-3911). Staff redirect these to the right level of care.

### 14.3 Location and Licensure

Telehealth is provided consistent with clinician licensure and applicable law, which can depend on where the patient is physically located at the time of the visit. Patients are asked to confirm their location at the start of the visit so care is delivered appropriately and safely.

## 15. Consent for Telehealth

### 15.1 Informed Consent

Before or at the start of a telehealth visit, the patient gives **informed consent** to telehealth, having been told: what telehealth is and its benefits and limitations; that a visit may need to convert to in-person care; how privacy and security are protected; how information is documented (in the same legal medical record, HIM-300); any cost considerations (FIN-700); and what to do if the technology fails or an emergency arises (call 911). Consent is documented.

### 15.2 Privacy in Telehealth

Telehealth uses secure, privacy-protective technology. Patients are encouraged to be in a private location for the visit, and clinicians confirm who else is present. The same HIPAA protections and minimum-necessary rules apply (Part 3).

### 15.3 Right to Decline

A patient may decline telehealth and request an in-person visit, and is offered one within the appropriate timeframe (SRA-250). Choosing or declining telehealth never penalizes the patient.

## 16. Technology Requirements and Support

### 16.1 What Patients Need

For a video visit, a patient generally needs a smartphone, tablet, or computer with a camera and microphone, an internet connection, and access through MyLRMC or the visit link. A **phone (audio-only) visit** is offered when video is not feasible, so that lack of video technology does not block access.

### 16.2 Help and the Digital Divide

The MyLRMC Help Desk (585-230-3000) and Virtual Care team (585-230-3500) help patients test and join visits in advance. Accessibility features (captioning, screen-reader-compatible materials) and language support (585-230-6400) are provided. Patients without suitable technology are offered audio-only visits or in-person care; telehealth is an option, never a requirement.

### 16.3 If the Technology Fails

If a video connection fails during a visit, the clinician switches to phone or reschedules promptly, and the patient is told what to expect. If an emergency arises during a telehealth visit, the patient is directed to call 911, and the clinician takes appropriate action under EUA-150.

## Part 6 of 7 — Complaints and Grievance Process; the Patient Advocate

## 17. The Patient Advocate

The **Patient Advocate / Office of Patient Experience (585-230-4000)** helps patients understand their rights, navigate concerns, and resolve complaints and grievances. The Patient Advocate is a neutral resource available to any patient or representative, works across departments to resolve issues, and ensures concerns are addressed without any effect on the patient's care. Patients are told about the Patient Advocate in patient materials, on request, and whenever they express a concern that is not resolved at the point of service.

## 18. Raising a Concern, Complaint, or Grievance

### 18.1 Concerns Resolved at the Point of Service

Many concerns are resolved promptly by the staff or supervisor present — for example, a scheduling mix-up or a question about care. Staff are empowered to address concerns immediately and to involve a supervisor or the Patient Advocate (585-230-4000) when needed.

### 18.2 Filing a Grievance

A **grievance** is a complaint that the patient wants formally investigated and answered, or that cannot be resolved promptly. Patients (or their representatives) may file a grievance:

- Through the MyLRMC portal,
- By contacting the Patient Advocate (585-230-4000) or Patient Relations (585-230-5500),
- By phone, in writing, or in person.

Interpretation (585-230-6400) and accessibility support (585-230-6450) are provided so any patient can file a grievance.

### 18.3 The Grievance Process

1. **Acknowledge** the grievance promptly (generally within a few business days) and explain the process and timeframe.
2. **Investigate** — the Patient Advocate coordinates a review with the relevant department(s), gathering the facts fairly.
3. **Resolve and respond in writing** — the patient receives a written response with the findings, any actions taken, and contact information, **generally within 7 business days**, or, when the matter is complex, an interim update with the expected completion (and resolution generally within 30 days).
4. **Escalate if unresolved** — the patient is told how to escalate within LRMC and is informed of their right to contact external oversight bodies (such as the state health department or the relevant accreditation organization) regardless of LRMC's process.

### 18.4 No Retaliation

Filing a complaint or grievance **never** affects the patient's access to or quality of care. Retaliation is prohibited, and the Patient Advocate ensures the patient's care continues normally throughout.

### 18.5 Privacy-Specific Complaints

Complaints about privacy or a possible breach are routed to the **Privacy Officer (585-230-4010)** and Compliance / Risk Management (585-230-8100), investigated, and answered, and patients are informed of their right to complain to federal authorities about privacy concerns. Patients are not retaliated against for raising a privacy concern.

### 18.6 Using Complaints to Improve

Complaints and grievances are reviewed by the PRPEC for patterns and improvement opportunities, so individual concerns lead to systemic improvements. Trends are reported to leadership and used to improve patient experience.

## 20. Common Patient Questions

### 20.1 "What are my rights as a patient?"

You have the right to respectful, nondiscriminatory care; to clear information and to take part in decisions; to give or refuse consent; to privacy and confidentiality; to access your records; to choose how we communicate with you; and to raise concerns or file a grievance without it affecting your care. You'll receive our Notice of Privacy Practices, and our Patient Advocate (585-230-4000) can help you exercise any of these rights.

### 20.2 "How is my health information protected?"

We protect your information under HIPAA and state law, use and share only the minimum necessary for permitted purposes, and require your authorization for most other uses. Sensitive information gets extra protection. You can access your records, request corrections, ask for an accounting of certain disclosures, and request confidential communications. Privacy concerns go to our Privacy Officer at 585-230-4010.

### 20.3 "Can my spouse, parent, or caregiver access my information?"

Yes, if they're set up as an authorized proxy, which we verify to protect your privacy. You choose the scope and can change it anytime. Special rules apply for minors and for sensitive information. To set up a proxy, contact the MyLRMC Help Desk at 585-230-3000.

### 20.4 "How do I sign up for or get help with MyLRMC?"

You can enroll online with identity verification or with help from our Help Desk at 585-230-3000. The portal lets you message your team, view results and records, schedule, request refills, and pay bills. If the portal isn't right for you, we'll help you by phone or in person. Please remember it's not for emergencies — call 911 for those.

### 20.5 "Who can use telehealth, and what do I need?"

Telehealth works for many visits that don't need an in-person exam — follow-ups, medication management, behavioral-health visits, and more. You'll need a phone, tablet, or computer with a camera and internet, or we can do an audio-only phone visit. We'll review consent and confirm your location at the start. It's not for emergencies. Contact LRMC Virtual Care at 585-230-3500 for help.

### 20.6 "Is telehealth private and secure?"

Yes. We use secure, privacy-protective technology, and the same HIPAA protections apply as for an in-person visit. We recommend being in a private space, and we'll confirm who's with you. Your visit is documented in your medical record just like any other visit.

### 20.7 "How do I make a complaint, and will it affect my care?"

You can share a concern with the staff present, or contact our Patient Advocate at 585-230-4000 to look into it, or file a formal grievance through the portal, by phone, or in writing. We'll acknowledge it promptly and respond in writing, usually within 7 business days. Filing a complaint will never affect your care — that's a firm commitment, and you can also contact outside oversight bodies if you wish.

### 20.8 "What if I don't use the internet or a smartphone?"

That's completely fine — the portal and telehealth are options, not requirements. You can do everything by phone or in person: schedule at 585-230-2600, get help at the Help Desk (585-230-3000), and have in-person visits. We also provide interpreters (585-230-6400) and accommodations (585-230-6450) so everyone can access care.

## 21. Quality Monitoring and Oversight

The PRPEC monitors rights, privacy, portal, telehealth, and grievance performance — privacy-incident and breach handling, portal enrollment and accessibility, telehealth utilization and conversion rates, grievance acknowledgment and resolution timeliness, and complaint themes — and reviews events through the non-punitive reporting system, escalating serious privacy events to the Privacy Officer (585-230-4010) and Compliance / Risk Management (585-230-8100). Metrics are reviewed for equity (language, disability, digital access), and findings drive improvements. Patient feedback is gathered with the Patient Advocate (585-230-4000).

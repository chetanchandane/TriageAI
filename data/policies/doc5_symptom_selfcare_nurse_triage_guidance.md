## Part 1 of 3 — How to Use This Guidance, Safety Rules, and the Universal Red-Flag Reminder

## 1. About This Guidance

### 1.1 Title and Identification

This is the **Symptom-Based Self-Care & Nurse-Triage Guidance** of Lakeside Regional Medical Center (LRMC), policy document **SYM-300**. It provides plain-language, generic self-care advice, warning signs, and "when to seek care" thresholds for common symptoms. It supports nurse triage, patient messaging, and the organization's automated triage-support assistant by giving consistent, policy-grounded content for the everyday symptoms patients ask about.

LRMC is a 400-bed community teaching hospital and outpatient network at 1200 Lakeshore Parkway, Rochester, NY 14600. The hospital main line is 585-230-0000. The **after-hours nurse line is 585-230-0911**. For any emergency, the instruction throughout this document is to **call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department**.

### 1.2 What This Guidance Is and Is Not

This guidance is **general health information**, not individualized medical advice. It is written to be safe for a broad audience and deliberately avoids specific medication doses; where a medication is mentioned, the instruction is to use it **as directed on the package label or by a clinician or pharmacist**. Self-care advice here applies to generally healthy adults with mild, typical symptoms. Patients who are pregnant, very young or very old, immunocompromised, or managing chronic conditions may need different guidance and should contact their care team. When this guidance is used to answer a patient, a qualified staff member reviews and approves any clinically significant reply before it is sent, consistent with PCT-100.

### 1.3 How the A–Z Library Works

Part 2 is a library of short, self-contained symptom sections, each named by the symptom and each following the same structure:

- A brief description of the symptom.
- **Self-care:** general measures a healthy adult can try for mild, typical symptoms.
- **Contact LRMC / seek care if:** the thresholds for a non-urgent visit, a same-day/urgent visit, or the nurse line.
- **Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** the red flags that require emergency care.

Each section is written to stand alone so it can be retrieved and used on its own. The red-flag (emergency) content always takes priority: if any emergency criterion is met, the patient is directed to emergency care regardless of the rest of the section.

## 2. Universal Safety Rules and Red-Flag Reminder

### 2.1 The Overriding Rule

When in doubt, treat the more serious possibility as real and seek care. No self-care advice in this document should ever be used to talk a patient out of seeking emergency care they feel they need. If a patient believes they are having an emergency, the answer is always: **call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department.**

### 2.2 Universal Emergency Red Flags (Apply to Every Symptom)

Regardless of the specific symptom, advise the patient to **call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department now** for any of the following:

- Chest pain or pressure, especially with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or pain spreading to the arm, jaw, neck, or back.
- Sudden face drooping, arm or leg weakness, trouble speaking, sudden confusion, sudden vision loss, or the worst headache of their life (signs of stroke).
- Severe difficulty breathing, inability to speak in full sentences, choking, or bluish lips.
- Swelling of the lips, tongue, or throat, or trouble breathing/swallowing after a trigger (severe allergic reaction).
- Severe bleeding that won't stop, vomiting or coughing up blood, or black/bloody stools with weakness.
- Fainting, a seizure, unresponsiveness, or being very difficult to wake.
- Thoughts of harming themselves or others, or an overdose/ingestion (also call the Behavioral Health Access Line, 585-230-3911).
- A symptom that is sudden, severe, "the worst ever," or rapidly worsening, or a strong sense that something is very wrong.

### 2.3 Who Needs a Lower Threshold to Seek Care

These groups should contact LRMC sooner and have a lower threshold for being seen: infants and young children, adults over 65, pregnant patients, people with weakened immune systems, and people with serious chronic conditions (heart, lung, kidney, liver disease, or diabetes). Self-care thresholds in Part 2 assume an otherwise-healthy adult; for these groups, advise earlier contact with the care team.

### 2.4 Where to Direct Patients

- **Emergency:** call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department.
- **Urgent, same-day, non-emergency:** Lakeside Urgent Care (585-230-1700) or a same-day visit.
- **After-hours clinical questions:** after-hours nurse line (585-230-0911).
- **Non-urgent visits:** Central Scheduling (585-230-2600) or LRMC Virtual Care (585-230-3500).
- **Medication questions/refills:** LRMC Outpatient Pharmacy (585-230-2100).
- **Poisoning/overdose (awake and breathing):** Poison Information Line (585-230-2700); if unresponsive or not breathing, call 911.

## Part 2 of 3 — A–Z Symptom Library

> Each section below gives general self-care for mild, typical symptoms in an otherwise-healthy adult, the thresholds for seeking care, and the red flags that require emergency care. The universal red flags in Section 2.2 apply to every symptom in addition to those listed.

### Abdominal pain (stomach pain)

Mild stomach pain is common and often from gas, indigestion, a stomach bug, constipation, or menstrual cramps.

**Self-care:** Rest; sip clear fluids; eat small, bland meals when able; use a heating pad for cramps; avoid alcohol, caffeine, and fatty or spicy foods until better. Over-the-counter remedies for gas, heartburn, or constipation may help mild cases when used as directed on the label.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Pain lasts more than a few days or keeps returning; pain with a fever; pain with vomiting or diarrhea that won't stop; burning with urination; pain that interferes with eating or sleeping; or you are pregnant. Call the nurse line (585-230-0911) after hours if you're unsure.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Sudden, severe, or rapidly worsening pain; a rigid, board-like abdomen; vomiting blood or material like coffee grounds; black or bloody stools with weakness or dizziness; pain with a high fever and confusion; severe pain in a pregnant patient; or pain with chest symptoms.

### Allergic reaction (mild)

A mild allergic reaction may cause localized hives, itching, sneezing, or a runny nose after exposure to a trigger.

**Self-care:** Avoid the trigger; cool compresses for itchy skin; an over-the-counter oral antihistamine may help when used as directed on the label. Watch closely for any spread or breathing symptoms.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Hives are widespread or keep coming back over days; the reaction doesn't improve with an antihistamine; or you have repeated reactions and don't know the cause and want evaluation.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Swelling of the lips, tongue, throat, or face; trouble breathing, swallowing, or a tight throat; widespread hives with faintness, vomiting, or a sense of doom; or any reaction in someone with a known severe allergy — use an epinephrine auto-injector if prescribed, then call 911. This is anaphylaxis and is an emergency.

### Anxiety and stress (mild)

Feeling worried, tense, or stressed is common. Mild, situational anxiety often improves with self-care.

**Self-care:** Regular sleep, physical activity, and limiting caffeine and alcohol; slow breathing and relaxation techniques; talking with someone you trust; and breaking problems into smaller steps. Reputable self-help resources are listed in the appendix.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Anxiety is persistent, interferes with daily life, work, or sleep, or comes with panic episodes; you want to talk to someone or explore treatment. LRMC Behavioral Health can help — call the Behavioral Health Access Line at 585-230-3911.

**Emergency — call 911 or 585-230-3911 now if:** You have thoughts of harming yourself or others, feel unable to stay safe, or have taken steps toward self-harm. You can also call or text the national crisis line. You are not alone, and help is available right now.

### Back pain (lower back)

Most lower-back pain is from muscle strain and improves within a few weeks.

**Self-care:** Stay gently active rather than bed rest; use heat or cold on the area; over-the-counter pain relievers as directed on the label; gentle stretching as tolerated; and good lifting posture. Most simple back strain improves with time.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Pain is severe, not improving after a couple of weeks, or keeps returning; pain spreads down a leg; or it follows a fall or injury without red-flag features.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Back pain with new loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness in the groin or inner thighs, or significant leg weakness; back pain after major trauma; back pain with fever and feeling very ill; or sudden severe back or flank pain with a pulsating sensation in an older adult. These can signal a serious problem.

### Bites and stings (insect)

Most insect bites and stings cause local redness, swelling, and itching that resolve in a few days.

**Self-care:** Remove a stinger if visible by scraping; wash with soap and water; cold compress for swelling; over-the-counter antihistamine or hydrocortisone cream for itching as directed; avoid scratching to prevent infection.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Increasing redness, warmth, or pus after a day or two (possible infection); a tick bite with later rash, fever, or joint aches; or a bite that isn't healing.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Signs of a severe allergic reaction — swelling of the lips/tongue/throat, trouble breathing, widespread hives with faintness. Use an epinephrine auto-injector if prescribed, then call 911.

### Burns (minor)

Minor (first-degree and small superficial second-degree) burns affect the top layers of skin with redness and mild blistering.

**Self-care:** Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 10–20 minutes; do not use ice; cover loosely with a clean, non-stick dressing; do not pop blisters; over-the-counter pain relief as directed. Keep it clean while it heals.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** A blistered burn larger than a few inches; signs of infection (increasing redness, pus, fever); a burn that isn't healing; or any burn in someone with diabetes or poor circulation.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** A large or deep burn; a burn on the face, hands, feet, genitals, or over a joint; a burn that looks white, charred, or leathery; an electrical or chemical burn; or any burn with trouble breathing or signs of smoke inhalation.

### Cold (common cold / upper respiratory infection)

A cold causes runny or stuffy nose, sore throat, cough, sneezing, and sometimes a mild fever, usually improving in 7–10 days.

**Self-care:** Rest; plenty of fluids; saline nasal spray or rinses; honey for cough (not for children under 1 year); a humidifier; and over-the-counter cold or pain remedies as directed on the label. Antibiotics do not help colds, which are viral.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Symptoms last beyond about 10 days or worsen after starting to improve; high or persistent fever; ear pain or facial/sinus pain and pressure; or you have a chronic lung condition.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Severe trouble breathing or shortness of breath at rest, chest pain, bluish lips, or confusion.

### Constipation

Constipation is infrequent or difficult bowel movements, often from low fiber, low fluids, inactivity, or some medications.

**Self-care:** Increase fiber (fruits, vegetables, whole grains); drink more water; stay active; and use an over-the-counter fiber supplement or gentle laxative as directed on the label if needed. Don't ignore the urge to go.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Constipation lasts more than a week despite self-care; it's a new change in your normal pattern; it alternates with diarrhea; or there's blood in the stool without red-flag features.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Severe abdominal pain with a swollen, hard belly and inability to pass stool or gas; vomiting with severe constipation; or black, tarry, or bloody stools with weakness or dizziness.

### Cough

A cough often follows a cold or irritation and usually improves over one to three weeks.

**Self-care:** Fluids; honey for soothing (not under age 1); a humidifier; lozenges or warm liquids; and over-the-counter cough remedies as directed on the label. Avoid smoke and irritants.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Cough lasts more than three weeks; cough with a fever that won't break; thick colored mucus with facial pain; wheezing; a known asthma or COPD flare; or coughing that disrupts sleep and isn't improving.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Severe shortness of breath, chest pain, bluish lips, coughing up more than a streak of blood, or trouble breathing that doesn't improve with a rescue inhaler.

### Cuts and scrapes (minor wounds)

Minor cuts and scrapes affect the skin surface and usually heal well with basic care.

**Self-care:** Wash your hands; stop bleeding with gentle pressure; clean the wound with water; apply a thin layer of plain petroleum jelly or antibiotic ointment and a clean bandage; change the dressing daily and keep it clean and dry.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** The wound is deep, gaping, or might need stitches; it was caused by a dirty or rusty object or an animal/human bite; you're unsure about tetanus vaccination; or signs of infection appear (increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, red streaks, or fever).

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Bleeding that won't stop with firm pressure, a deep wound with heavy bleeding, a wound exposing deeper tissue, or a wound from a major injury.

### Dental pain (toothache)

Tooth pain may come from a cavity, infection, a cracked tooth, or gum problems.

**Self-care:** Rinse with warm salt water; gentle flossing to remove trapped food; over-the-counter pain relief as directed; a cold compress on the outside of the cheek for swelling. See a dentist for the underlying cause.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Pain is severe or persistent; there's facial swelling; or you have a fever — a dental infection may need prompt treatment. Contact your dentist; LRMC can advise on urgent options.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Facial swelling that affects breathing or swallowing, spreads toward the eye, or comes with a high fever and feeling very ill — a spreading dental infection can be dangerous.

### Diarrhea

Diarrhea is loose, watery stools, often from a viral or foodborne illness, usually resolving in a few days.

**Self-care:** Drink plenty of fluids to stay hydrated (water, broth, oral rehydration solutions); eat bland foods as tolerated; avoid alcohol, caffeine, and very fatty or sugary foods. Wash hands well to avoid spreading illness.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Diarrhea lasts more than a few days; signs of dehydration (very dark urine, dizziness, dry mouth); a fever; blood or black stools without red-flag features; recent travel or antibiotic use; or you have a chronic condition.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Signs of severe dehydration (confusion, fainting, no urination), severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting with inability to keep any fluids down, or significant blood in the stool with weakness.

### Dizziness and lightheadedness

Dizziness can come from inner-ear issues, dehydration, standing up quickly, low blood sugar, or medications.

**Self-care:** Sit or lie down until it passes; stand up slowly; hydrate; eat regularly; avoid sudden head movements if it's positional. Note what triggers it.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Dizziness is frequent, persistent, or interfering with daily life; it comes with ear symptoms or hearing changes; or it may be related to a medication.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Dizziness with chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting, a severe headache, face drooping, weakness or numbness on one side, trouble speaking, double vision, or trouble walking — these can signal a stroke or heart problem.

### Ear pain

Ear pain may come from infection, fluid, wax buildup, or a cold, and is common in children.

**Self-care:** Over-the-counter pain relief as directed; a warm compress on the ear; keep the ear dry. Do not insert cotton swabs or objects into the ear canal.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Ear pain with fever; drainage from the ear; hearing loss; pain that lasts more than a day or two; or recurrent ear infections. Young children with ear pain and fever should be evaluated.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Severe pain with high fever, stiff neck, and confusion; swelling, redness, and tenderness of the bone behind the ear; or sudden severe dizziness with vomiting.

### Eye redness or irritation (including pink eye)

Red, irritated, or watery eyes may be from conjunctivitis (pink eye), allergies, or irritation.

**Self-care:** Cool or warm compresses; over-the-counter lubricating (artificial tear) drops; avoid touching/rubbing; wash hands often and don't share towels (pink eye can be contagious); remove contact lenses until better.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Thick colored discharge, eyelids stuck together, pain, or light sensitivity; symptoms lasting more than a few days; or a contact-lens wearer with a painful red eye (seek care promptly).

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, a chemical splash to the eye (rinse with water immediately and seek care), an eye injury, or a red eye with severe headache and vomiting.

### Fatigue (tiredness)

Feeling tired can come from poor sleep, stress, overwork, illness, or sometimes a medical condition.

**Self-care:** Prioritize regular, adequate sleep; balanced meals and hydration; regular activity; limit alcohol and excess caffeine; and manage stress. Mild fatigue from a busy stretch often improves with rest.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Fatigue is persistent (weeks), unexplained, worsening, or comes with other symptoms like weight changes, shortness of breath, or low mood; or it interferes with daily life.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Fatigue with chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting, or confusion.

### Fever (adult)

Fever is the body's response to infection and is usually not dangerous on its own in a healthy adult.

**Self-care:** Rest; drink plenty of fluids; dress lightly; and use an over-the-counter fever reducer as directed on the label if uncomfortable. Most viral fevers resolve in a few days.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Fever lasts more than three days; very high fever; fever with a productive cough, ear or sinus pain, burning urination, or other localizing symptoms; or you have a chronic condition or weakened immune system (seek care sooner).

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Fever with a stiff neck, severe headache, and light sensitivity; confusion or difficulty waking; trouble breathing; a non-blanching rash (spots that don't fade when pressed); a fever with severe weakness, fast breathing, or feeling like you might die (possible sepsis).

### Fever (child)

Most childhood fevers are from viral illness; how the child looks and acts matters more than the exact number.

**Self-care:** Keep the child comfortable and hydrated with frequent small drinks; dress lightly; use a children's fever reducer dosed by weight per the package or your pediatric clinician — never give aspirin to a child. Offer rest.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Fever in a child that lasts more than a couple of days; fever with ear pain, sore throat, or painful urination; a child who is more irritable or sleepy than usual; or poor fluid intake. When unsure, call the nurse line (585-230-0911).

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Any fever in an infant under 3 months; trouble breathing; a stiff neck or a rash that doesn't fade when pressed; a seizure; difficulty waking or unusual floppiness; no wet diapers/urination or other signs of dehydration; or a child who looks very ill.

### Flu-like illness (influenza / respiratory virus)

Flu-like illness causes fever, body aches, headache, fatigue, cough, and sore throat, usually improving over about a week.

**Self-care:** Rest; fluids; over-the-counter fever and pain relief as directed; stay home to avoid spreading illness. Antiviral treatment is most useful early and for higher-risk people — contact your care team promptly if you're higher risk.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** You're in a higher-risk group (older adult, pregnant, chronic illness, weakened immunity) and want early evaluation; symptoms are severe or not improving after a week; or symptoms improve then worsen again (possible secondary infection).

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Trouble breathing or shortness of breath, chest pain or pressure, persistent confusion, bluish lips, or severe weakness.

### Headache

Most headaches are tension-type or migraine and are not dangerous, though some patterns need evaluation.

**Self-care:** Rest in a quiet, dark room; hydrate; over-the-counter pain relief as directed on the label; manage stress and screen time; keep regular sleep and meals. Limit frequent use of pain medication, which can cause rebound headaches.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Headaches are frequent, worsening, or different from your usual pattern; they interfere with daily life; over-the-counter relief isn't working; or you're using pain medication very often.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** A sudden, severe "worst headache of my life"; headache with fever and a stiff neck; headache with face drooping, weakness, numbness, trouble speaking, vision loss, or confusion; headache after a head injury; or a headache with seizure or fainting.

### Heartburn and indigestion

Heartburn is a burning feeling in the chest or upper abdomen, often after meals, from acid reflux.

**Self-care:** Eat smaller meals; avoid trigger foods (spicy, fatty, acidic), alcohol, and late-night eating; don't lie down right after eating; raise the head of the bed; over-the-counter antacids or acid reducers as directed on the label.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Heartburn happens more than twice a week, isn't controlled by over-the-counter remedies, or comes with difficulty or pain swallowing, unintended weight loss, or vomiting.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Chest pain or pressure, especially with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw — heart problems can feel like indigestion. When in doubt, treat chest discomfort as a possible heart problem and call 911.

### Heat-related illness (heat exhaustion)

Heat exhaustion can occur with heat and exertion, causing heavy sweating, weakness, headache, nausea, and dizziness.

**Self-care:** Move to a cool place; rest; sip cool water or an electrolyte drink; loosen clothing; cool the skin with damp cloths or a fan. Symptoms should improve within an hour.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Symptoms don't improve with cooling and rest within about an hour, or you have a chronic condition that heat affects.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Signs of heat stroke — confusion, fainting, seizures, very high body temperature, hot/red skin (with or without sweating), or no improvement with cooling. Heat stroke is life-threatening; begin cooling and call 911.

### Hemorrhoids

Hemorrhoids are swollen veins around the anus that can cause itching, discomfort, and bright-red bleeding with bowel movements.

**Self-care:** High-fiber diet and plenty of fluids to keep stools soft; warm sitz baths; over-the-counter hemorrhoid creams or wipes as directed; avoid straining and prolonged sitting on the toilet.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Bleeding persists or recurs; symptoms don't improve with self-care; you have a change in bowel habits; or you're over 45 and haven't had recommended colon screening — rectal bleeding should be evaluated rather than assumed to be hemorrhoids.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Heavy rectal bleeding, black or tarry stools, or bleeding with dizziness, weakness, or fainting.

### Insomnia and sleep problems

Trouble falling or staying asleep is common and often related to stress, habits, caffeine, or screens.

**Self-care:** Keep a consistent sleep schedule; a cool, dark, quiet room; limit caffeine, alcohol, and screens before bed; avoid long daytime naps; get daytime activity; and use the bed only for sleep. These habits ("sleep hygiene") help most mild insomnia.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Insomnia lasts more than a few weeks, affects your daytime functioning or mood, or is tied to anxiety, depression, snoring/breathing pauses, or a medication. Behavioral Health can help (585-230-3911).

**Emergency — call 911 or 585-230-3911 now if:** Sleeplessness comes with thoughts of self-harm or a mental-health crisis.

### Joint pain

Joint pain may come from overuse, minor injury, arthritis, or inflammation.

**Self-care:** Rest the joint; ice for new injuries and heat for stiffness; over-the-counter pain relief as directed; gentle movement as tolerated; supportive footwear and activity changes.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Pain persists beyond a couple of weeks, recurs, or limits daily activities; there's swelling or stiffness in multiple joints; or it follows an injury without deformity.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** A single joint that is suddenly very red, hot, swollen, and painful, especially with fever (possible joint infection); inability to bear weight or use the limb after an injury; or a joint that looks deformed or dislocated.

### Menstrual cramps

Cramping with periods is common and usually manageable.

**Self-care:** Heating pad on the lower abdomen; gentle exercise; over-the-counter pain relievers as directed on the label, ideally started when cramps begin; rest and hydration.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Cramps are severe, worsening over time, or not relieved by usual measures; periods are very heavy or irregular; or pain occurs outside your period. LRMC women's-health services can help.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Severe pelvic pain with fainting, fever, or heavy bleeding; severe pain with a positive or possible pregnancy (possible ectopic pregnancy); or soaking through pads very rapidly with weakness.

### Muscle strain or sprain

Strains (muscle/tendon) and sprains (ligament) cause pain, swelling, and stiffness after overuse or a minor injury.

**Self-care:** Use R.I.C.E. — Rest, Ice (20 minutes at a time for the first day or two), Compression with an elastic wrap, and Elevation. Over-the-counter pain relief as directed; gradually return to activity as it improves.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Significant swelling or bruising; little improvement after a few days; pain that limits use of the limb; or repeated injuries.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Inability to bear weight or use the limb, an obvious deformity or possible broken bone, numbness, or a cold/pale limb after the injury.

### Nasal congestion and sinus symptoms

Stuffy nose and sinus pressure often accompany colds and allergies.

**Self-care:** Saline nasal spray or rinses; steam or a humidifier; plenty of fluids; over-the-counter decongestants or antihistamines as directed on the label (limit decongestant nasal sprays to a few days to avoid rebound congestion).

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Symptoms last beyond about 10 days or worsen after improving; facial pain/pressure with a fever; or thick colored discharge with significant pain (possible sinus infection).

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Sinus symptoms with high fever, severe headache, stiff neck, vision changes, or swelling/redness around the eye.

### Nausea and vomiting

Nausea and vomiting are common with stomach bugs, food issues, migraines, or medications.

**Self-care:** Sip small amounts of clear fluids frequently; rest the stomach, then reintroduce bland foods slowly; avoid strong odors, fatty or spicy foods, alcohol, and caffeine. Ginger or oral rehydration solutions may help.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Vomiting lasts more than a day or two; you can't keep fluids down; signs of dehydration appear; vomiting with a fever or significant pain; or you're pregnant or have a chronic condition.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Vomiting blood or material like coffee grounds; severe abdominal pain; vomiting with a severe headache, stiff neck, or confusion; signs of severe dehydration (fainting, no urination, confusion); or vomiting after a head injury.

### Nosebleed

Most nosebleeds come from the front of the nose and stop with simple measures.

**Self-care:** Sit up and lean slightly forward (not back); pinch the soft part of the nose firmly for 10–15 minutes without releasing; breathe through the mouth; apply a cold compress to the bridge. Afterward, avoid blowing or picking the nose and use a little petroleum jelly or a humidifier for dryness.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Nosebleeds are frequent or recurrent; they follow starting a new medication (such as a blood thinner); or they're hard to control but not severe.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Bleeding won't stop after 20–30 minutes of firm pressure; very heavy bleeding or a large amount of blood; a nosebleed after a significant injury; or bleeding with lightheadedness, weakness, or trouble breathing.

### Rash

Rashes have many causes — irritation, allergic reactions, heat, infections, or skin conditions — and most mild rashes are not dangerous.

**Self-care:** Avoid known irritants; use gentle, fragrance-free cleansers and moisturizers; cool compresses; over-the-counter hydrocortisone cream or oral antihistamine for itching as directed; avoid scratching. Keep the area clean and dry.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** A rash spreads, blisters, oozes, or doesn't improve in a week; it comes with a fever; it's painful; or it follows a new medication (possible drug reaction — contact your care team).

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** A rash with swelling of the lips/tongue/throat or trouble breathing (allergic emergency); a rash that doesn't fade when pressed with a fever and feeling very ill (possible meningitis/sepsis); widespread blistering or peeling skin; or a rapidly spreading, very painful red area with fever.

### Seasonal allergies (hay fever)

Allergies to pollen, dust, or pets cause sneezing, itchy or watery eyes, and a runny or stuffy nose.

**Self-care:** Limit exposure (close windows on high-pollen days, shower after being outdoors); saline rinses; over-the-counter antihistamines, nasal steroid sprays, or allergy eye drops as directed on the label. Symptoms are often seasonal and predictable.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Symptoms aren't controlled with over-the-counter measures; they interfere with sleep or daily life; or you also have asthma that flares with allergies.

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Trouble breathing, wheezing that doesn't respond to a rescue inhaler, or swelling of the lips/tongue/throat.

### Sore throat

Most sore throats are viral and improve in a few days; some are from strep or other causes.

**Self-care:** Warm liquids; honey (not under age 1); salt-water gargles; lozenges; fluids and rest; over-the-counter pain relief as directed on the label.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Severe or persistent sore throat (beyond a few days); sore throat with a fever and swollen glands but no cough (possible strep, which may need testing); white patches on the tonsils; or a rash with the sore throat.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Trouble breathing or swallowing, drooling with inability to swallow, a muffled "hot potato" voice, severe one-sided throat pain with neck swelling, or swelling of the tongue or throat.

### Sunburn

Sunburn is skin damage from UV exposure, causing redness, warmth, and sometimes blistering.

**Self-care:** Cool compresses or a cool bath; moisturizer or aloe; over-the-counter pain relief as directed; drink fluids; stay out of the sun until healed; don't pop blisters. Prevent future burns with sunscreen and protective clothing.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Extensive blistering, signs of infection, or sunburn with significant pain that isn't improving.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Sunburn with confusion, fainting, high fever, or signs of heat stroke; or very extensive blistering with feeling very ill.

### Urinary symptoms (urinary tract infection, UTI)

A UTI commonly causes burning with urination, frequency, urgency, and lower-abdominal discomfort.

**Self-care:** Drink plenty of water; urinate when you feel the urge; over-the-counter urinary pain relievers may ease burning temporarily (they don't treat the infection). A UTI usually needs evaluation and may require prescription treatment, so contact your care team.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Burning, frequency, or urgency that suggests a UTI (often needs assessment and possible treatment); blood in the urine; symptoms in someone who is pregnant, male, a child, or has diabetes (seek care sooner); or recurrent UTIs.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Urinary symptoms with a high fever, shaking chills, flank or back pain, nausea/vomiting, or confusion — this can mean a kidney infection or sepsis and needs prompt care; or an inability to urinate at all with pain.

### Vaginal irritation or discharge (including yeast infection)

Itching, irritation, or changes in vaginal discharge can come from yeast infections, bacterial imbalance, or irritation.

**Self-care:** Avoid douching, scented products, and irritants; wear breathable cotton underwear; for a typical yeast infection, an over-the-counter antifungal used as directed may help. If symptoms are new, unclear, or recurrent, seek evaluation rather than self-treating.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** It's your first episode or you're unsure of the cause; symptoms don't improve with over-the-counter treatment; there's a foul odor, colored discharge, pelvic pain, or fever; possible exposure to a sexually transmitted infection; or you're pregnant.

**Emergency — call 911 or go to the ED now if:** Severe pelvic or abdominal pain with fever, fainting, or heavy bleeding, or symptoms with a possible pregnancy and severe pain.

### Wheezing (mild)

Wheezing is a whistling sound when breathing, often from asthma, allergies, or a respiratory infection.

**Self-care:** If you have asthma, use your prescribed rescue inhaler as directed and follow your asthma action plan; avoid known triggers; sit upright and try to stay calm. Mild, occasional wheezing that responds to a rescue inhaler can often be managed at home with your plan.

**Contact LRMC / seek care if:** Wheezing is new and unexplained; it's happening more often; your rescue inhaler is needed more than usual; or you have a known asthma/COPD condition that's becoming harder to control (see CDM-350 and contact your care team).

**Emergency — call 911 now if:** Severe shortness of breath, inability to speak in full sentences, wheezing that doesn't improve with a rescue inhaler, bluish lips, or chest tightness with breathing difficulty.

## Part 3 of 3 — Common Patient Questions (FAQ), Documentation, and Appendices

## 3. Common Patient Questions

These are phrased the way patients ask them, with policy-grounded answers staff can adapt. Each stands alone for retrieval.

### 3.1 "Can I treat this at home or should I be seen?"

For many mild, typical symptoms in an otherwise-healthy adult, home care is reasonable to start — rest, fluids, and over-the-counter remedies used as directed. You should be seen if symptoms are severe, last longer than expected, keep coming back, or come with warning signs for that symptom. And for any emergency sign — trouble breathing, chest pain, signs of a stroke, severe bleeding, or thoughts of self-harm — call 911 right away. If you're unsure, our after-hours nurse line (585-230-0911) can help you decide.

### 3.2 "How long should I wait before contacting you?"

It depends on the symptom, but a good rule is to contact us if a symptom is severe, isn't improving in the expected timeframe, is getting worse, or comes with a warning sign. People who are older, pregnant, immunocompromised, or managing a chronic condition should reach out sooner. Never wait on emergency signs — call 911.

### 3.3 "What over-the-counter medicine should I take?"

For general guidance, use over-the-counter products as directed on the package label, and ask our pharmacy (585-230-2100) if you're unsure which product is right for you or whether it's safe with your other medications. We avoid giving specific doses by message because the right choice depends on your health, age, other medications, and the product. Never give aspirin to children.

### 3.4 "Are antibiotics going to help?"

Often not — many common illnesses like colds, most sore throats, the flu, and most coughs are caused by viruses, which antibiotics don't treat. Antibiotics help specific bacterial infections, and using them when they're not needed can cause side effects and resistance. If your symptoms suggest a bacterial infection, we'll evaluate and prescribe appropriately.

### 3.5 "When is a fever dangerous?"

In a healthy adult, a fever itself usually isn't dangerous and is the body fighting infection. Seek care if a fever lasts more than three days, is very high, or comes with localizing symptoms. Call 911 for a fever with a stiff neck and severe headache, confusion, trouble breathing, or a rash that doesn't fade when pressed. Any fever in an infant under 3 months needs emergency evaluation.

### 3.6 "I have a UTI — can you just call in an antibiotic?"

Urinary symptoms usually need a quick assessment first, and sometimes a urine test, to confirm a UTI and choose the right treatment, so we'll have you connect with a clinician (a video visit often works). Drink plenty of water meanwhile. Seek urgent care if you develop fever, back/flank pain, or vomiting, which can mean a kidney infection.

### 3.7 "Should I use ice or heat?"

For a fresh injury, sprain, or strain, ice in the first day or two helps reduce swelling. For stiff, sore muscles or chronic aches, heat is usually more soothing. Use either for about 20 minutes at a time with a barrier against the skin, and stop if it increases pain.

### 3.8 "My symptom isn't on your list — what do I do?"

This guidance covers common symptoms, but it can't list everything. If your symptom is severe, sudden, or worrying, or you have any emergency sign, call 911. Otherwise, send us a message or call — describe what's going on and how long it's lasted — and our team will help you figure out the right next step. After hours, call the nurse line at 585-230-0911.

## 5. Appendices

### 5.1 Appendix A — Universal Red Flags (Call 911 / ED), Any Symptom

- Chest pain/pressure, especially with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or arm/jaw pain.
- Sudden face droop, arm/leg weakness, speech trouble, confusion, vision loss, or worst-ever headache (stroke).
- Severe trouble breathing, can't speak in sentences, choking, or blue lips.
- Lip/tongue/throat swelling or trouble breathing/swallowing (anaphylaxis).
- Severe bleeding that won't stop; vomiting/coughing blood; black or bloody stools with weakness.
- Fainting, seizure, unresponsiveness, or very hard to wake.
- Thoughts of self-harm or harming others, or an overdose (also 585-230-3911).
- Any sudden, severe, "worst-ever," or rapidly worsening symptom, or a sense that something is very wrong.

### 5.2 Appendix B — Where to Direct Patients

- **Emergency:** 911 or nearest emergency department.

## Part 1 of 8 — Purpose, Scope, Governance, Definitions, and the Refill Request Lifecycle

## 1. About This Manual

### 1.1 Title and Identification

This is the **Medication Management and Prescription Refill Policy Manual** of Lakeside Regional Medical Center (LRMC). It is policy document **RX-500** within the LRMC Administrative Policy Library, and it is the authoritative reference for how prescription refills are requested, reviewed, authorized, dispensed, and communicated across the Medical Center and its affiliated outpatient network.

Lakeside Regional Medical Center is a 400-bed community teaching hospital and affiliated outpatient network at 1200 Lakeshore Parkway, Rochester, NY 14600. The hospital main line is 585-230-0000. The **LRMC Outpatient Pharmacy** main line is **585-230-2100**, and all pharmacy-related lines use the **585-230-21XX** range, listed in full in Section 5.5 and repeated wherever a contact is relevant.

### 1.2 Why This Manual Exists

Prescription refills are among the highest-volume requests Lakeside Regional Medical Center receives, arriving through the MyLRMC patient portal, by telephone, from retail and mail-order pharmacies, and in person. A safe, consistent refill process protects patients from running out of needed medication, prevents inappropriate or duplicate prescribing, supports responsible stewardship of controlled substances, and gives staff clear, defensible rules for the questions patients ask most often.

This manual exists to:

1. **Keep patients safely supplied.** Patients on chronic medications should not experience preventable gaps in therapy because of avoidable processing delays.
2. **Keep prescribing safe and appropriate.** Every refill is an opportunity to confirm that the medication remains correct, that required monitoring is current, and that the patient is being seen as needed.
3. **Steward controlled substances responsibly.** Opioids, benzodiazepines, stimulants, and other controlled medications carry specific legal requirements and elevated risk, and are handled under the dedicated rules in Part 3.
4. **Give staff and patients clear expectations.** Defined turnaround times, eligibility rules, and message scripts make handling consistent regardless of which staff member or clinic processes the request.

### 1.3 How to Use This Manual

Each numbered section is written to stand on its own. If you need only the rule for early refills, the turnaround time for a routine refill, or the contact for prior authorization, you should be able to act from that single section without reading the entire manual. Cross-references point you to related detail, but the essential rule is always restated where it is used.

Timeframes in this manual (for example, "routine refill requests are processed within 2 business days") are **policy standards**. Departures must be documented with the reason, following the documentation rules in Section 4.6 and the LRMC documentation policy HIM-300.

### 1.4 Relationship to Other LRMC Policies

This manual works alongside the following related policies. Where a conflict exists, the more protective patient-safety standard governs, and the conflict is reported to the Pharmacy and Therapeutics Committee (Section 4.1).

- **PCT-100 — Patient Communication and Triage Policy.** Governs how portal messages, including refill requests, are received, triaged, and documented. A refill request that also describes a new or worsening symptom is triaged for its clinical content first under PCT-100.
- **PCT-210 — Telephone Triage and Nurse Advice Line Policy.** Governs spoken refill and medication requests, including after-hours handling via the nurse line at 585-230-0911.
- **HIM-300 — Documentation and the Legal Medical Record.** Defines what is documented and how.
- **PRIV-400 — Patient Privacy and Confidential Communications.** Governs identity verification, proxy access, and protection of sensitive medication information.
- **BH-600 — Behavioral Health Access and Suicide-Risk Response.** Governs medication requests connected to behavioral-health crisis, intentional misuse, or overdose risk (Behavioral Health Access Line, 585-230-3911).
- **CS-550 — Controlled Substance Management and Diversion Prevention.** The institutional controlled-substance policy that Part 3 of this manual operationalizes for outpatient refills.

### 1.5 A Note on Medical Advice

This manual is an operational policy document. Nothing in it constitutes individualized medical advice, and no timeframe, dose, or instruction in this manual should be applied to a specific patient without the judgment of that patient's prescriber. Patient-facing messages produced under this manual are reviewed and approved by qualified staff before release, consistent with PCT-100.

## 2. Scope and Applicability

### 2.1 What This Manual Covers

This manual applies to the **outpatient prescription refill and medication-management process** at Lakeside Regional Medical Center, including:

- Refill requests submitted through the **MyLRMC** patient portal and mobile application.
- Refill requests received by telephone, including the pharmacy refill line at 585-230-2101.
- Electronic refill requests transmitted to LRMC from external retail and mail-order pharmacies.
- Renewal of expired prescriptions and prescriptions with no remaining refills, which require prescriber review.
- Controlled-substance prescriptions, subject to the additional rules in Part 3.
- Prior-authorization and formulary-coverage handling (Part 5).
- Specialty, high-cost, and limited-distribution medications (Part 6).
- Patient communications about any of the above (Part 7).

### 2.2 What This Manual Does Not Cover

This manual does **not** govern:

- **Inpatient medication ordering, administration, or reconciliation**, which are governed by the LRMC inpatient pharmacy and nursing policies.
- **New prescriptions for new conditions**, which require an appropriate clinical encounter rather than a refill workflow, although this manual describes when a refill request must be converted into a visit.
- **Medication-assisted treatment (MAT) program rules** that have their own protocols under behavioral health and addiction services, except where those programs reference the controlled-substance handling in Part 3.
- **Immunizations and vaccines**, governed by the LRMC immunization policy.
- **Medical-device and durable-medical-equipment supplies** (for example, glucometers and CPAP supplies) except where a prescription is involved.
- **Billing disputes** about medication charges, which route to Patient Financial Services (585-230-7000), although coverage and prior-authorization questions affecting whether a medication can be filled are addressed in Part 5.

### 2.3 Who Must Follow This Manual

This manual applies to every LRMC workforce member who receives, reviews, authorizes, dispenses, or communicates about prescription refills, including:

- Prescribers (physicians, nurse practitioners, physician assistants) who authorize refills and renewals.
- Pharmacists and pharmacy technicians at the LRMC Outpatient Pharmacy (585-230-2100) and Specialty Pharmacy (585-230-2110).
- Registered nurses and licensed practical nurses who triage and process refill messages under approved protocols.
- Medical assistants and clinical support staff who gather information and route refill requests.
- Prior-authorization and pharmacy-benefit staff (585-230-2120).
- Clinic managers and supervisors who oversee refill queues and turnaround performance.
- Temporary, per-diem, contract, and student personnel with access to refill workflows, after completing required training.

### 2.4 Patient Eligibility for Portal Refills

Refill requests through MyLRMC are available to established LRMC patients (and authorized proxies under PRIV-400) for medications originally prescribed by an LRMC prescriber. A patient may also request that LRMC assume management of a medication started elsewhere, but that request is handled as a clinical matter requiring prescriber review, not a simple refill. The portal refill function displays, at the point of request, the standing notice:

> Refill requests submitted here are reviewed during business hours. Allow up to 2 business days for routine refills. **If you have already run out of an essential medication, or this is an emergency, do not wait for a portal reply** — call the Outpatient Pharmacy at 585-230-2100, or for a medical emergency call 911.

## 3. Guiding Principles for Medication Management

The following principles govern every refill decision. When a situation is not specifically addressed later, staff apply these principles.

### 3.1 Continuity of Essential Therapy

Patients on essential chronic medications — for example, medications for cardiovascular disease, seizure disorders, diabetes, asthma, thyroid disease, organ-transplant immunosuppression, and mental-health conditions — should not experience preventable interruptions. When a request involves an essential medication and the patient is at or near running out, staff prioritize the request and, when appropriate, arrange a short bridge supply under the rules in Part 2 and Part 4 while the full review is completed.

### 3.2 Every Refill Is a Clinical Checkpoint

A refill is not a clerical act. Each request is reviewed against the patient's record to confirm that the medication is still indicated, that the dose and directions are current, that required laboratory or other monitoring is up to date, and that the patient is being seen at the appropriate interval. When monitoring or a visit is overdue, the refill workflow may provide a limited bridge supply while the patient is scheduled, rather than indefinitely refilling without follow-up.

### 3.3 Right Authority for Each Decision

Only a prescriber, or staff acting under a specific approved protocol, authorizes a refill. Pharmacy technicians and clinical support staff prepare and route requests but do not make prescribing decisions. Controlled-substance refills carry tighter authority limits, defined in Part 3.

### 3.4 Stewardship of Controlled Substances

Opioids, benzodiazepines, stimulants, and other controlled substances are managed conservatively, consistent with state and federal law, the New York State Prescription Monitoring Program registry check requirements, and LRMC policy CS-550. Stewardship protects patients from harm and the community from diversion, and it is never overridden for convenience. Part 3 governs these medications in detail.

### 3.5 Safety-Netting and Clear Instructions

Patients are told plainly what was done with their request, when and where to pick up or expect the medication, what to do if they run out before it is ready, and what symptoms or changes should prompt them to seek care. Medication replies follow the safety-netting standard of PCT-100 and the scripts in Part 7.

### 3.6 Privacy and Sensitivity

Medication lists can reveal sensitive diagnoses. Staff verify identity and proxy authority under PRIV-400 before discussing medications, share only the minimum necessary information, and apply additional care to sensitive categories, including behavioral-health, reproductive-health, HIV, and substance-use-treatment medications.

### 3.7 Document Every Decision

Every refill decision — approval, denial, modification, bridge supply, or conversion to a visit — is documented in the legal medical record with the reason, consistent with HIM-300. An undocumented refill decision is treated, for safety and legal purposes, as if it did not occur.

## 4. Governance, Roles, and Accountability

### 4.1 Policy Ownership and Oversight

This manual is owned by the **Pharmacy and Therapeutics (P&T) Committee** of Lakeside Regional Medical Center, in coordination with the Patient Communication Oversight Committee for all patient-messaging aspects. The P&T Committee is chaired by the Director of Pharmacy and includes prescriber, nursing, pharmacy, compliance, and patient-safety representation. The committee reviews this manual at least **annually**, and sooner when law, formulary, or safety data require it. The committee can be reached through the Director of Pharmacy's office at 585-230-2100.

### 4.2 Roles and Responsibilities

**Prescriber (Responsible Provider).** Authorizes refills and renewals, makes prescribing decisions, reviews monitoring and follow-up needs, and is the only role that may authorize a controlled-substance prescription. Reachable through the patient's clinic.

**Pharmacist.** Performs clinical review of medications for appropriateness, interactions, and safety; counsels patients; supervises dispensing; and serves as the escalation point for medication-safety concerns. LRMC Outpatient Pharmacy, 585-230-2100.

**Pharmacy Technician.** Receives and prepares refill requests, processes insurance claims, coordinates pickup and mailing, and routes requests requiring clinical review to the pharmacist or prescriber. Does not make clinical decisions. Refill line, 585-230-2101.

**Triage Nurse (RN/LPN).** Reviews refill messages with clinical content, applies approved nurse protocols, identifies requests that require prescriber action, and arranges bridge supplies where protocol allows. Coordinates with the prescriber for anything outside protocol.

**Clinical Support Staff (MA).** Gathers information needed to process a refill (for example, current pharmacy, last fill date, recent symptoms) and routes the request. Does not authorize refills or give medication advice.

**Prior-Authorization / Pharmacy-Benefit Staff.** Manages insurance prior authorizations, formulary exceptions, and coverage appeals. Prior Authorization Unit, 585-230-2120.

**Specialty Pharmacy Staff.** Coordinates specialty, high-cost, and limited-distribution medications, including enrollment, financial assistance, and shipment. Specialty Pharmacy, 585-230-2110.

**Clinical Pharmacist / Medication Therapy Management (MTM).** Provides comprehensive medication review, anticoagulation and other disease-state management, and complex refill problem-solving. MTM line, 585-230-2130.

**Clinic Manager / Supervisor.** Monitors refill-queue volumes and turnaround performance, manages coverage and reassignment, and supports staff on difficult requests.

**Compliance / Risk Management.** Advises on controlled-substance, privacy, and diversion questions and receives related reports. Reachable at 585-230-8100.

### 4.3 Accountability for Every Refill Request

Each refill request has an identifiable **owner** at all times — the staff member or role responsible for the next action. Ownership transfers explicitly when a request is routed or escalated, and the handoff is documented. No refill request is left unowned in a queue. Supervisors audit daily for aging or ownerless requests, as described in Part 8.

### 4.4 Delegation Under Approved Protocols

Certain routine refill actions may be performed by trained nursing or pharmacy staff under written protocols approved by the P&T Committee and the relevant medical director — for example, processing an on-schedule refill of a stable, non-controlled chronic medication with current monitoring. Protocols define exactly which medications, conditions, and limits apply. Anything outside a protocol returns to the prescriber. No protocol authorizes refilling a controlled substance without prescriber action.

### 4.5 Non-Punitive Reporting

LRMC maintains a non-punitive culture for reporting refill errors, near-misses, and diversion concerns. Staff who identify a wrong-medication, wrong-dose, missed-monitoring, or potential-diversion issue report it through the safety-event system so the organization can correct and learn. Honest reporting is protected.

### 4.6 Documentation Standard for Refill Decisions

Every refill decision is documented in the legal medical record at the time it is made, including: the medication, strength, and directions; the action taken (approved, denied, modified, bridged, or converted to a visit); the reason; any monitoring or follow-up required; the controlled-substance registry check where applicable (Part 3); and the patient communication sent. Documentation follows HIM-300 and is contemporaneous, not retrospective.

## 5. Definitions

Each definition is written to stand alone so a reader encountering the term in any Part can resolve it here.

**Refill.** The dispensing of an additional supply of a medication under an existing, valid prescription that has remaining authorized fills. A refill does not require a new prescribing decision unless review reveals a reason for one.

**Renewal (re-authorization).** The issuance of a new authorization for a medication whose prescription has expired or has no remaining refills. A renewal is a prescribing decision and requires prescriber review, even when the medication is unchanged.

**Bridge supply (bridge fill).** A limited, short-duration quantity of an essential non-controlled medication provided under protocol to prevent a therapy gap while a full review, overdue monitoring, or an appointment is completed. Bridge supplies are defined and limited in Part 2 and Part 4 and do not apply to controlled substances except under the narrow prescriber-authorized circumstances in Part 3.

**Controlled substance.** A medication classified under federal and New York State law in Schedules II through V because of its potential for misuse or dependence — including most opioids, benzodiazepines, and stimulants. Controlled substances are governed by Part 3 and LRMC policy CS-550.

**Schedule II medication.** A controlled substance with high misuse potential (for example, many opioid pain medications and certain stimulants) that, under law, **cannot be refilled** — each fill requires a new prescription — and is subject to the strict rules in Part 3.

**Early refill.** A request to fill a medication before enough of the prior supply has elapsed to expect it to be exhausted at the prescribed directions. Early refills are scrutinized, especially for controlled substances, under Part 4.

**Prior authorization (PA).** A requirement imposed by a patient's insurer that coverage of a specific medication be approved in advance based on clinical criteria. The PA process is described in Part 5 and handled by the Prior Authorization Unit, 585-230-2120.

**Formulary.** The list of medications a patient's insurance plan covers, often in tiers. A non-formulary medication may require a formulary exception or prior authorization, addressed in Part 5.

**Specialty medication.** A high-cost, often injectable or biologic medication that typically requires special handling, monitoring, enrollment, or limited-distribution sourcing — for example, many medications for autoimmune disease, multiple sclerosis, hepatitis C, and certain cancers. Specialty medications are addressed in Part 6 and coordinated by the Specialty Pharmacy, 585-230-2110.

**PMP / registry check.** A check of the New York State Prescription Monitoring Program registry, required before prescribing certain controlled substances, used to review a patient's controlled-substance history. Required steps are in Part 3.

**Days' supply.** The number of days a dispensed quantity is expected to last at the prescribed directions. Days' supply is used to determine when a refill is due and whether a request is early (Part 4).

**Mail-order / external pharmacy.** A pharmacy other than the LRMC Outpatient Pharmacy where a patient chooses to have prescriptions filled. LRMC transmits prescriptions electronically to these pharmacies; turnaround for the patient also depends on that pharmacy's processing.

**Proxy.** An individual authorized under PRIV-400 to manage a patient's medications and communications on their behalf, such as a parent of a minor or caregiver of a dependent adult. Proxy status is verified before medication information is shared.

**Legal medical record.** The official, permanent record of the patient's care, governed by HIM-300, in which refill decisions and communications are documented. Health Information Management, 585-230-4200.

## 6. The Refill Request Lifecycle — Overview

This section gives the high-level flow of a refill request through Lakeside Regional Medical Center, from arrival to closure. Each stage is summarized here and detailed in later Parts, so a reader who needs only the big picture can rely on this section alone.

### 6.1 The Seven Stages at a Glance

1. **Request submitted.** The patient submits a refill request through MyLRMC, by phone (585-230-2101), in person, or via an external pharmacy's electronic request.
2. **Intake and identity check.** Staff confirm the request is from the patient or a verified proxy and capture the medication, strength, directions, and preferred pharmacy.
3. **Clinical and safety review.** Staff and/or a pharmacist verify the medication is current and appropriate, check remaining refills and last fill date, confirm monitoring and follow-up status, and screen for early-refill, controlled-substance, or interaction concerns.
4. **Authorization decision.** A prescriber, or protocol-authorized staff, approves, modifies, denies, bridges, or converts the request to a visit.
5. **Dispensing or transmission.** The medication is dispensed by the LRMC Outpatient Pharmacy or the prescription is transmitted to the patient's chosen pharmacy.
6. **Patient communication.** The patient is told what was done, when and where to obtain the medication, what to do if they run out first, and any safety-netting, using the scripts in Part 7.
7. **Documentation and closure.** The decision and communication are documented per HIM-300, and the request is closed only when the medication is on its way to the patient or the patient has been safely scheduled or redirected.

### 6.2 Stage 1 — Request Submitted

A patient may request a refill through MyLRMC, the staffed refill line (585-230-2101), in person at the Outpatient Pharmacy (585-230-2100), or by asking an external pharmacy to transmit a request to LRMC. Each channel feeds the same review process. The portal displays the standing notice that routine refills take up to 2 business days and that a patient who has already run out of an essential medication should call the pharmacy rather than wait (Section 2.4).

### 6.3 Stage 2 — Intake and Identity Check

Before processing, staff confirm the request is from the patient or a proxy authorized under PRIV-400, and capture the essential details: the medication name, strength, and directions; the prescriber; the preferred pickup or mailing pharmacy; and the date of the last fill if known. If a portal refill message also reports a new or worsening symptom, the symptom is triaged first under PCT-100 before the refill is processed.

### 6.4 Stage 3 — Clinical and Safety Review

Staff and, where required, a pharmacist verify that the medication is still indicated; that the dose and directions match the current record; that there are remaining authorized refills or that a renewal is needed; that required monitoring (for example, laboratory tests) and follow-up visits are current; and that there is no early-refill, duplicate-therapy, interaction, or controlled-substance concern. Controlled substances additionally require the registry check described in Part 3.

### 6.5 Stage 4 — Authorization Decision

A prescriber, or staff acting under an approved protocol within its limits, makes the decision: **approve** as requested; **modify** (for example, adjust quantity or directions); **deny** with a documented reason and a patient-appropriate explanation; **bridge** with a limited supply while a visit or monitoring is arranged; or **convert** the request into an appointment because a clinical encounter is required. Only a prescriber may authorize a controlled-substance prescription.

### 6.6 Stage 5 — Dispensing or Transmission

If LRMC will fill the medication, the Outpatient Pharmacy dispenses it and prepares it for pickup or mailing. If the patient uses an external or mail-order pharmacy, the prescription is transmitted electronically; staff inform the patient that the external pharmacy's own processing time then applies.

### 6.7 Stage 6 — Patient Communication

The patient is told, in plain language, what was done, when and where to obtain the medication, what to do if they will run out before it is ready, and any relevant safety information — for example, monitoring that must be completed or symptoms that warrant urgent care. Communications use the approved scripts in Part 7 and follow the human-review requirement of PCT-100: a qualified staff member reviews and approves any clinically significant medication reply before it is sent.

### 6.8 Stage 7 — Documentation and Closure

The decision, the reason, any monitoring or follow-up required, the controlled-substance registry check where applicable, and the patient communication are documented in the legal medical record per HIM-300. The request is closed only when the medication is dispensed or transmitted, or the patient has been safely scheduled or redirected. A request that requires follow-up is not closed by a reply alone.

### 6.9 Where the Automated Assistant Fits

Lakeside Regional Medical Center's automated triage-support assistant may help by screening refill messages, identifying the medication and request type, flagging possible safety concerns (such as a co-reported symptom or an early-refill pattern), and proposing a policy-grounded draft reply for staff review. The assistant **supports** the workflow; it does not authorize refills and does not replace prescriber or pharmacist judgment. Two rules always apply, restated wherever the assistant is mentioned:

1. **Human-in-the-loop is mandatory.** A qualified staff member reviews and approves any clinically significant medication reply before it is sent, and only a prescriber or protocol-authorized staff member authorizes a refill.
2. **The assistant fails safe.** If it is uncertain or unavailable, the standard human workflow handles the request with no loss of safety; a flag raises a request for human review and never auto-approves a medication.

## Part 2 of 8 — Standard Refill Procedures, Request Channels, and Turnaround-Time Standards

## 7. Request Channels and How Each Is Handled

Lakeside Regional Medical Center accepts refill requests through four channels. All four feed the same clinical-review and authorization process described in Part 1; they differ only in intake. This section describes each channel so that staff handling any one of them can act correctly from this section alone.

### 7.1 MyLRMC Patient Portal

The MyLRMC patient portal is the preferred channel for routine, non-urgent refills. A patient selects the medication from their active medication list, confirms the pharmacy, and submits the request. The portal timestamps the request and routes it to the responsible clinic's refill queue. Portal requests are reviewed during business hours (Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. Eastern, excluding LRMC-observed holidays). A request submitted after hours or on a weekend is treated as received at the start of the next business day for the purpose of turnaround standards. The portal displays the standing notice that routine refills take up to 2 business days and that a patient who has already run out of an essential medication should call the Outpatient Pharmacy at 585-230-2100 rather than wait.

### 7.2 Telephone — Staffed and Automated Refill Line

The pharmacy refill line is **585-230-2101**. During business hours it is staffed by pharmacy technicians; outside business hours it accepts automated refill requests that enter the queue for the next business day. Telephone requests are entered into the same refill queue as portal requests and follow the same review. Staff taking a telephone request capture the patient's identity per PRIV-400, the medication and strength, the pharmacy, and any patient-reported concern. A caller who reports a new or worsening symptom is transferred to nurse triage under PCT-210 rather than handled as a simple refill, and a caller describing an emergency is directed to call 911.

### 7.3 In Person at the Outpatient Pharmacy

Patients may request refills in person at the LRMC Outpatient Pharmacy (585-230-2100). In-person requests follow the same review and authorization process. When a patient presents in person and is at immediate risk of running out of an essential medication, pharmacy staff prioritize the request and coordinate a bridge supply where protocol allows (Section 9.5), rather than sending the patient away to wait the standard turnaround.

### 7.4 Electronic Requests from External Pharmacies

Retail and mail-order pharmacies transmit electronic refill-authorization requests to LRMC when a patient asks them to fill a medication that has no remaining refills. These requests arrive in the clinic refill queue and are reviewed like any other. When LRMC authorizes the refill, the authorization is transmitted back to the requesting pharmacy electronically; the patient then obtains the medication from that pharmacy, and that pharmacy's own processing time applies in addition to LRMC's review time. Staff communicating with the patient make this two-step timing clear (Part 7).

### 7.5 Channels That Are Not Accepted for Refills

To keep refill handling safe and auditable, LRMC does **not** accept refill requests through unsecured email, social-media messages, voicemail left on non-pharmacy clinic lines, or messages to individual staff members' direct lines. Patients who attempt these channels are redirected to the portal or the refill line (585-230-2101). This restriction is stated to patients in the scripts in Part 7.

## 8. The Standard Refill Procedure, Step by Step

This section gives the standard operating procedure for a routine, non-controlled refill. Controlled substances follow the additional steps in Part 3; early or lost-medication requests follow Part 4; medications needing insurance approval follow Part 5.

### 8.1 Step 1 — Verify the Requester

Confirm the request comes from the patient or a proxy authorized under PRIV-400. If proxy authority is unclear, complete identity verification before releasing any medication information. Do not disclose a medication list, fill history, or denial reason to anyone whose authority has not been verified.

### 8.2 Step 2 — Match the Request to the Record

Locate the medication on the patient's active medication list and confirm the strength and directions match the request. If the patient requests a medication that is not on the active list, or requests a different strength or directions, the request is not a simple refill: it is routed to the prescriber as a possible change requiring a clinical decision.

### 8.3 Step 3 — Check Remaining Fills and Last Fill Date

Determine whether the prescription has remaining authorized refills and when it was last filled. If refills remain and the timing is consistent with the days' supply, the request proceeds. If no refills remain or the prescription has expired, the request becomes a **renewal**, which requires prescriber review even if the medication is unchanged (Section 8.7).

### 8.4 Step 4 — Confirm Monitoring and Follow-Up Status

Check whether the medication requires monitoring (for example, periodic laboratory tests, blood-pressure checks, or a follow-up visit) and whether that monitoring is current. Common examples include kidney-function and electrolyte monitoring for certain blood-pressure medications, thyroid-level monitoring for thyroid medication, and INR monitoring for warfarin through the Anticoagulation Clinic (585-230-2140). If monitoring or a required visit is overdue, the request is handled under the bridge-supply and scheduling rules in Section 9.5 rather than refilled indefinitely.

### 8.5 Step 5 — Screen for Safety Concerns

Screen the request for early-refill timing, duplicate therapy, significant interactions, recent hospitalization or medication changes, and any controlled-substance involvement. Any of these flags routes the request to a pharmacist or prescriber for review rather than routine processing. A co-reported symptom is triaged under PCT-100 first.

### 8.6 Step 6 — Authorize, Modify, Deny, Bridge, or Convert

The prescriber, or protocol-authorized staff within protocol limits, records one of five dispositions:

1. **Approve** as requested.
2. **Modify** the quantity, strength, or directions, with the reason documented.
3. **Deny**, with a documented reason and a patient-appropriate explanation prepared for the reply (Part 7).
4. **Bridge** with a limited supply while monitoring or a visit is arranged (Section 9.5).
5. **Convert** the request into an appointment because a clinical encounter is required.

Only a prescriber may authorize a controlled-substance prescription.

### 8.7 Step 7 — Handle Renewals That Need Prescriber Review

When a prescription has expired or has no remaining refills, the request is a renewal and is routed to the responsible prescriber. The prescriber confirms the medication remains appropriate, that monitoring and follow-up are adequate, and either renews the prescription or converts the request into a visit. Renewals are completed within the renewal turnaround standard in Section 9.3.

### 8.8 Step 8 — Dispense or Transmit

For medications LRMC fills, the Outpatient Pharmacy dispenses and prepares the medication for pickup or mailing. For external or mail-order pharmacies, the authorization is transmitted electronically, and the patient is told that the receiving pharmacy's processing time then applies.

### 8.9 Step 9 — Communicate and Document

The patient is informed of the outcome using the approved scripts in Part 7, including what was done, when and where to obtain the medication, what to do if they run out first, and any monitoring or safety-netting. The decision and the communication are documented in the legal medical record per HIM-300. The request is closed only when the medication is dispensed or transmitted or the patient is safely scheduled or redirected.

## 9. Turnaround-Time Standards

This section defines how quickly each type of refill request is handled. These are **policy standards**; a departure must be documented with the reason. All "business day" measures use LRMC ambulatory business hours (Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m. Eastern, excluding LRMC-observed holidays), and a request received after hours is treated as received at the start of the next business day.

### 9.1 Routine, Non-Controlled Refills

A routine refill of a stable, non-controlled medication with remaining authorized refills and current monitoring is processed **within 2 business days** of receipt. "Processed" means a disposition has been recorded and, where approved, the medication has been dispensed or the authorization transmitted. Patients are advised to submit routine refill requests at least **5 business days** before they expect to run out, to allow for review and for any pharmacy processing on the patient's end.

### 9.2 Requests Requiring Prescriber Review (No Refills Remaining)

A refill that requires prescriber review because no refills remain or the prescription has expired — a **renewal** — is completed **within 3 business days** of receipt, provided no clinical visit or additional information is required. If the prescriber determines a visit or new information is needed, the request is converted to that pathway and the patient is notified within the same 3-business-day window.

### 9.3 Requests Requiring Monitoring or a Visit

When a refill cannot be completed because monitoring or a visit is overdue, staff notify the patient **within 2 business days** of what is needed and how to complete it, arrange the needed laboratory test or appointment, and, for essential medications, provide a bridge supply under Section 9.5 so therapy is not interrupted while the patient completes the requirement.

### 9.4 Requests Requiring Insurance Prior Authorization

When a medication requires insurance prior authorization, the refill cannot be completed until the insurer approves coverage. Staff begin the prior-authorization process **within 2 business days** of identifying the requirement and keep the patient informed of status. Because the insurer controls the approval timeline, the total time is not fully within LRMC's control; the prior-authorization process and its communication standards are detailed in Part 5, and the Prior Authorization Unit is reachable at 585-230-2120.

### 9.5 Bridge Supplies for Essential Medications

When a patient is at or near running out of an **essential non-controlled** medication and the full review, monitoring, or appointment cannot be completed in time, protocol-authorized staff may arrange a limited **bridge supply** — generally enough to cover the patient until the requirement is met, and typically not exceeding a short interval defined by the governing protocol — while the patient is promptly scheduled. Bridge supplies are documented with the reason and the follow-up plan. Bridge supplies do **not** apply to controlled substances except under the narrow prescriber-authorized circumstances in Part 3.

### 9.6 Urgent Refill Needs

If a patient reports they have already run out of, or will within 24 hours run out of, an essential medication, the request is handled on an **expedited** basis — the same business day when received during business hours, and through the after-hours nurse line (585-230-0911) when received outside business hours for genuinely time-sensitive essential medications. Staff do not ask a patient in this situation to wait the standard 2-business-day window. Patients are told to call the Outpatient Pharmacy at 585-230-2100 rather than rely on a portal reply when they have already run out.

### 9.7 When a Standard Cannot Be Met

If a turnaround standard cannot be met — for example, because the prescriber is unavailable or information is missing — staff notify the patient of the delay and the expected timeframe within the original standard's window, take interim action to protect an essential medication (such as a bridge supply), and document the reason for the delay. A missed standard is never handled silently.

## 10. Special Procedures Within Standard Refills

### 10.1 Pharmacy-Change Requests

When a patient asks to change the pharmacy where a medication is filled, staff verify identity per PRIV-400, update the preferred pharmacy in the record, and confirm with the patient which pending and future fills the change applies to. A pharmacy change does not by itself authorize a new fill; the underlying refill rules still apply.

### 10.2 Multiple Medications in One Request

When a single request lists several medications, each medication is evaluated on its own against the rules above — one may be approved immediately while another requires renewal, monitoring, or prior authorization. The patient is told the status of each medication so that no item is silently dropped. The most time-sensitive essential medication sets the priority for the request.

### 10.3 Recently Hospitalized or Recently Changed Patients

A refill request from a patient recently discharged from the hospital, seen in the Emergency Department, or known to have had a recent medication change receives pharmacist or prescriber review before routine processing, because the medication list may have changed. Staff reconcile the request against the most recent documented regimen rather than an outdated list.

### 10.4 Sensitive-Category Medications

Medications that can reveal sensitive conditions — including behavioral-health, reproductive-health, HIV, and substance-use-treatment medications — receive the additional privacy handling in PRIV-400. Staff share only the minimum necessary information, confirm the correct and authorized recipient before any disclosure, and follow any patient-designated confidential-communication preferences.

### 10.5 Patients Who Use Mail-Order for Maintenance Medications

Many patients fill chronic maintenance medications through a mail-order pharmacy in 90-day supplies. For these patients, staff confirm that enough refills are authorized to avoid a gap, that the mail-order pharmacy has the current prescription, and that the patient understands mail-order shipping time. When a maintenance prescription is nearing expiration, staff proactively route it for renewal under Section 8.7 so the mail-order supply is not interrupted.

## Part 3 of 8 — Controlled-Substance Rules and Opioid/Narcotic Stewardship

## 11. Scope and Principles for Controlled Substances

### 11.1 What This Part Governs

This Part governs the refill, renewal, and patient communication for **controlled substances** prescribed through Lakeside Regional Medical Center's outpatient network — medications classified under federal and New York State law in Schedules II through V, including most opioid pain medications, benzodiazepines and related sedative-hypnotics, stimulants used for attention and related conditions, and other scheduled medications. It operationalizes the institutional controlled-substance policy CS-550 for the outpatient refill setting. Where this Part and CS-550 differ, the more protective standard governs, and the difference is reported to the Pharmacy and Therapeutics Committee. The Controlled-Substance Compliance / Diversion line is **585-230-2150**.

### 11.2 Core Principles

Controlled-substance handling at LRMC rests on five principles, each restated wherever it applies:

1. **Legal compliance is non-negotiable.** Federal and New York State controlled-substance law set hard limits — for example, Schedule II medications cannot be refilled and require a new prescription for each fill. No LRMC workflow, script, or convenience overrides the law.
2. **Only a prescriber authorizes a controlled substance.** No nurse protocol, pharmacy technician action, or automated tool authorizes a controlled-substance prescription. Support staff and the automated assistant may prepare and route, but never authorize.
3. **Stewardship protects patients and the community.** Conservative prescribing, registry checks, and diversion vigilance protect patients from harm and reduce the risk of misuse and diversion.
4. **Continuity still matters.** Patients legitimately treated with controlled substances — including those on stable long-term therapy — are treated with respect and are not abruptly cut off; gaps and abrupt discontinuation can themselves cause harm. Concerns are addressed through the prescriber and, where indicated, behavioral-health or addiction services (Behavioral Health Access Line, 585-230-3911).
5. **Document everything.** Every controlled-substance decision, registry check, and patient communication is documented in the legal medical record per HIM-300.

### 11.3 The Patient-Facing Stance

Staff communicate controlled-substance rules to patients factually and without judgment. The rules exist for safety and legal compliance, not suspicion of the individual. Scripts in Part 7 model neutral, respectful phrasing for the most common situations — for example, explaining that a pain medication cannot be refilled by message and requires a prescriber visit.

---

## 12. Legal and Regulatory Requirements

### 12.1 Schedule II Medications Cannot Be Refilled

Under federal law, Schedule II controlled substances (including many opioid pain medications and certain stimulants) **cannot be refilled**. Each fill requires a separate, newly issued prescription. A patient request to "refill" a Schedule II medication through the portal or refill line is therefore handled as a request for a **new prescription**, which requires prescriber review and is subject to the visit and monitoring requirements in Section 13. Staff never tell a patient a Schedule II medication will be "refilled"; the correct language is that a new prescription may be issued by the prescriber if appropriate.

### 12.2 Electronic Prescribing of Controlled Substances

LRMC transmits controlled-substance prescriptions electronically through its compliant electronic-prescribing system, consistent with New York State's electronic-prescribing requirements. Paper prescriptions for controlled substances are issued only in the narrow circumstances permitted by law and CS-550 (for example, a documented technological failure), and only by the prescriber.

### 12.3 Prescription Monitoring Program (Registry) Check

Before issuing a controlled-substance prescription where required by New York State law, the prescriber consults the New York State Prescription Monitoring Program (PMP) registry to review the patient's controlled-substance history. The registry check, and the prescriber's review of it, are documented in the legal medical record. Support staff may not substitute for the prescriber's review of the registry.

### 12.4 Quantity, Duration, and Day-Supply Limits

Controlled-substance prescriptions follow the quantity and duration limits set by law, by the patient's insurer, and by LRMC clinical protocols — including limits applicable to initial opioid prescriptions for acute pain. Staff do not exceed these limits, and requests that would exceed them are routed to the prescriber for a documented clinical decision.

### 12.5 Refills Available for Some Lower-Schedule Medications

Certain Schedule III–V controlled substances may be dispensed with a limited number of refills within the period the law allows. Even where refills are legally permitted, LRMC applies its stewardship review (Section 13) before each authorization, and a prescriber remains the only authority for the prescription.

---

## 13. Stewardship Requirements Applied to Refill Requests

### 13.1 Prescriber Review of Every Controlled-Substance Request

Every controlled-substance refill or new-prescription request is reviewed by the responsible prescriber. There is no protocol-only or auto-approval pathway for controlled substances. Support staff and pharmacy technicians gather information, perform identity verification under PRIV-400, and route the request; the prescriber makes the decision.

### 13.2 Controlled-Substance Agreements and Treatment Plans

Patients on chronic controlled-substance therapy (for example, long-term opioid therapy) are typically managed under a documented treatment plan or controlled-substance agreement that may include defined visit intervals, registry checks, periodic toxicology screening where clinically indicated, a single-prescriber and single-pharmacy expectation, and safe-storage and disposal counseling. When a refill request arrives, staff confirm that the patient is current with the terms of that plan; gaps are routed to the prescriber rather than resolved by a refill.

### 13.3 Required Visit Intervals

Controlled-substance prescriptions are tied to defined in-person or telehealth follow-up intervals appropriate to the medication and condition. When a refill request arrives and the patient is due or overdue for a follow-up, the request is converted to an appointment under the prescriber's direction. Staff schedule the visit promptly (Central Scheduling, 585-230-2600) and inform the patient that the prescription depends on completing it.

### 13.4 No Routine Bridge Supplies for Controlled Substances

The bridge-supply mechanism that protects essential non-controlled medications (Part 2, Section 9.5) does **not** apply to controlled substances as a routine matter. A short interim supply of a controlled substance may be issued only by the prescriber, as a documented clinical decision, and never under a support-staff protocol or automated action. Staff do not promise a patient a bridge supply of a controlled substance.

### 13.5 Single-Prescriber and Single-Pharmacy Expectations

For chronic controlled-substance therapy, LRMC generally expects a single responsible prescriber and a single dispensing pharmacy, both documented. Requests that would create overlapping prescriptions, multiple prescribers, or multiple pharmacies for the same controlled medication are routed to the prescriber for review, and the registry is consulted.

### 13.6 Safe Use, Storage, Disposal, and Naloxone

Consistent with opioid stewardship, prescribers and pharmacists counsel patients on safe use, secure storage away from others (especially children), and proper disposal of unused controlled medications, and offer naloxone where clinically appropriate for patients on opioid therapy or at elevated overdose risk. Patient questions about disposal and naloxone are answered using the scripts in Part 7 and the resources in the Part 8 appendix.

---

## 14. Handling Specific Controlled-Substance Situations

### 14.1 "Please Refill My Pain Medication" by Portal Message

A portal request to refill an opioid or other Schedule II pain medication cannot be completed as a message-based refill. Staff route it to the prescriber, who determines whether a new prescription is appropriate, whether the patient is due for a visit or registry review, and whether the request fits the treatment plan. The patient receives a neutral reply explaining that the medication requires prescriber review and, typically, an appointment, and is given the path to schedule it (Part 7). Staff do not deny or approve such a request on their own.

### 14.2 Early Requests for Controlled Substances

A request to refill a controlled substance earlier than the days' supply would predict is a flag and is always routed to the prescriber, who reviews the registry and the treatment plan before any decision. Early-request handling for controlled substances is stricter than for non-controlled medications; the general early-refill rules are in Part 4, but for controlled substances the prescriber-review requirement is absolute.

### 14.3 Reports of Lost, Stolen, or Damaged Controlled Substances

A report that a controlled substance was lost, stolen, or damaged is handled under Part 4's lost-medication procedure **and** the controlled-substance rules here: the report is routed to the prescriber, documented in detail (what happened, when, and the patient's account), and the registry is reviewed. Replacement of a lost or stolen controlled substance is a prescriber decision made case by case, is not guaranteed, and is documented with the rationale. Repeated reports are reviewed for a pattern and may prompt a care-plan discussion or referral to behavioral-health or addiction services (585-230-3911).

### 14.4 Possible Misuse, Diversion, or Substance-Use Concerns

If any staff member identifies signs of possible misuse or diversion — for example, conflicting reports, registry findings suggesting multiple prescribers, or requests inconsistent with the treatment plan — the concern is routed to the prescriber and reported through the Controlled-Substance Compliance / Diversion line (585-230-2150) consistent with CS-550. Concerns are handled clinically and respectfully; a patient who may have a substance-use disorder is offered help, not simply refused, and is connected to behavioral-health or addiction services (585-230-3911). Suspected diversion by a staff member is reported under CS-550 to Compliance / Risk Management (585-230-8100).

### 14.5 Tapering and Discontinuation Requests

Requests related to tapering or discontinuing a controlled substance are clinical decisions for the prescriber and are not handled by routine refill processing. Abrupt discontinuation can cause harm; staff route these requests to the prescriber and do not adjust or stop a controlled medication by message.

### 14.6 Travel and Early-Supply Needs Involving Controlled Substances

A patient who needs a controlled-substance supply aligned to travel is handled by the prescriber, who considers legal limits, the treatment plan, and the registry. The general vacation/travel-supply mechanism in Part 4 applies to non-controlled medications; for controlled substances, only the prescriber may decide, and the decision is documented.

## 15. Documentation for Controlled Substances

Every controlled-substance interaction is documented in the legal medical record per HIM-300 and CS-550, including: the request and channel; identity verification; the prescriber's review and decision; the registry (PMP) check and the prescriber's review of it where required; the quantity, strength, and directions authorized; any treatment-plan or agreement references; counseling provided (safe use, storage, disposal, naloxone); and the patient communication sent. For lost/stolen/damaged reports and possible-diversion concerns, the documentation additionally records the patient's account, the actions taken, and any reports made to the Compliance / Diversion line (585-230-2150).

## Part 4 of 8 — Early Refills, Lost/Stolen/Damaged Medication, and Vacation/Travel Supplies

## 16. Early-Refill Policy

### 16.1 What Counts as an Early Refill

An **early refill** is a request to fill a medication before enough time has elapsed, at the prescribed directions, to expect the prior supply to be used up. LRMC evaluates timing against the documented days' supply and last fill date. Early requests are reviewed rather than auto-processed because they can signal a dose change, a lost supply, a coverage reset, an upcoming travel need, or — for controlled substances — a safety concern.

### 16.2 Standard Early-Refill Window for Non-Controlled Medications

For routine non-controlled maintenance medications, LRMC generally allows a refill when most of the current supply has been used — commonly when roughly three-quarters of the days' supply has elapsed — so that patients can refill a few days before running out without creating an unsafe surplus. Requests earlier than this window are reviewed for the reason. The exact threshold can vary by medication and by the patient's insurance, which enforces its own early-refill limits (Section 16.5).

### 16.3 Legitimate Reasons for an Early Non-Controlled Refill

Staff approve or route early non-controlled requests based on the reason, which is documented. Common legitimate reasons include a prescriber-directed dose increase (more medication used per day), synchronization of multiple medications to a single monthly pickup, an upcoming trip (Section 18), a recent lost or damaged supply (Section 17), or a transition between pharmacies. When the reason is a dose change, the request is routed to the prescriber to update the prescription rather than simply dispensing more of the old directions.

### 16.4 Early Refills for Controlled Substances

Early requests for **controlled substances** are always routed to the prescriber, who reviews the registry (PMP) and the treatment plan before any decision, as required by Part 3. There is no support-staff or automated early-refill pathway for controlled substances. Staff do not approve, deny, or promise an early controlled-substance refill; only the prescriber decides, and the decision is documented.

### 16.5 Insurance Early-Refill Limits

A patient's insurer typically will not pay for a refill filled too soon, independent of LRMC's clinical view. When an early refill is clinically appropriate but the insurer rejects it as too soon, staff explain the insurer's limit to the patient, determine whether a one-time override is available (for example, for travel or a dose change), and, where needed, coordinate with the Prior Authorization Unit (585-230-2120) or advise the patient that they may pay out of pocket if they choose. The interplay of clinical approval and insurance coverage is covered further in Part 5.

### 16.6 Documentation of Early Refills

Every early-refill decision is documented with the reason, the disposition, and — for controlled substances — the prescriber's review and registry check. Repeated early requests are reviewed for a pattern; for controlled substances this review is mandatory and may prompt a treatment-plan discussion (Part 3).

## 17. Lost, Stolen, or Damaged Medication

### 17.1 General Approach

Patients sometimes report that medication was lost, spilled, damaged (for example, left in a hot car or destroyed in a household accident), or stolen. LRMC handles these reports compassionately and consistently. The handling differs sharply between non-controlled and controlled medications, and staff apply the correct path based on the medication.

### 17.2 Lost or Damaged Non-Controlled Medication

For a non-controlled medication, staff verify the patient's identity (PRIV-400), confirm the medication and the circumstances, and — when the medication is essential and the patient is now without it — arrange a replacement supply under the standard refill rules, including a bridge supply where appropriate (Part 2, Section 9.5). The report and action are documented. Insurance may not pay for an early replacement; staff explain this and, where needed, coordinate an override or advise the patient of out-of-pocket options (Section 16.5).

### 17.3 Lost or Stolen Controlled Medication

For a **controlled substance**, a lost or stolen report is routed to the prescriber and handled under Part 3, Section 14.3. The report is documented in detail (what happened, when, and the patient's account), the registry (PMP) is reviewed, and replacement is a case-by-case prescriber decision that is not guaranteed. Staff do not promise replacement of a lost or stolen controlled substance. A report of theft is documented factually; the patient may be advised that theft of a controlled substance can be reported to law enforcement, and repeated reports are reviewed for a pattern (Part 3).

### 17.4 Damaged Medication Returned to the Pharmacy

When a patient brings damaged medication to the LRMC Outpatient Pharmacy (585-230-2100), pharmacy staff document the condition and follow CS-550 for any controlled substance, including witnessed handling and disposal where required. Replacement still follows the rules above — routine for non-controlled essential medications, prescriber decision for controlled substances.

### 17.5 Documentation for Lost/Stolen/Damaged Reports

All such reports are documented in the legal medical record, including the medication, the patient's account of what happened, the date, the action taken, and — for controlled substances — the prescriber's decision, the registry review, and any report to the Compliance / Diversion line (585-230-2150).

## 18. Vacation, Travel, and Extended-Absence Supplies

### 18.1 Planning Ahead

Patients traveling or temporarily relocating may need medication beyond their normal pickup schedule. LRMC encourages patients to request travel supplies **at least 2 weeks** before departure so review, any insurance override, and pharmacy processing can be completed. The travel-supply scripts in Part 7 set this expectation.

### 18.2 Vacation Overrides for Non-Controlled Medications

For non-controlled maintenance medications, staff can often arrange a "vacation override" so the patient has enough supply for the trip. This may involve dispensing an additional supply within the available refills, requesting an insurance vacation override for an early fill, or, where appropriate, a prescriber adjusting the prescription to a larger supply. The reason (travel) is documented, and the patient is told the expected timing and any out-of-pocket considerations if the insurer will not cover the early fill.

### 18.3 Travel Supplies of Controlled Substances

Travel supplies of **controlled substances** are decided only by the prescriber, who considers legal quantity and duration limits, the treatment plan, and the registry (Part 3, Section 14.6). Staff do not promise a travel supply of a controlled substance. Patients traveling with controlled medications are advised to keep them in the original labeled container and to check the rules of their destination, especially for international travel; detailed guidance is referenced in the Part 8 appendix.

### 18.4 Extended Domestic or International Relocation

A patient relocating for an extended period (for example, students or seasonal residents) may need a plan for ongoing care and prescriptions in another location. Staff route these requests to the prescriber and, where appropriate, to care coordination, so the patient is connected to a local provider rather than relying indefinitely on long-distance refills. Mail-order arrangements (Part 2, Section 10.5) may help for stable non-controlled maintenance medications.

### 18.5 Documentation of Travel and Vacation Supplies

Travel and vacation-supply decisions are documented with the reason, the quantity authorized, any insurance override obtained, and — for controlled substances — the prescriber's decision and registry review.

## Part 5 of 8 — Prior Authorization, Formulary, and Insurance-Coverage Process

## 19. Understanding Prior Authorization and Formulary

### 19.1 What Prior Authorization Is

A **prior authorization (PA)** is a requirement imposed by a patient's insurer that coverage of a specific medication be approved in advance, based on the insurer's clinical criteria. A PA is an **insurance-coverage** decision, not a clinical decision by LRMC: the prescriber may consider a medication appropriate, yet the insurer still requires approval before it will pay. The LRMC Prior Authorization Unit manages this process and is reachable at **585-230-2120**.

### 19.2 What a Formulary Is

A **formulary** is the list of medications a patient's plan covers, usually arranged in tiers that affect the patient's cost. A **non-formulary** medication may not be covered at all without a formulary exception, and a higher-tier medication may cost the patient more. When a prescribed medication is non-formulary or high-tier, staff consider whether a covered therapeutic alternative is clinically appropriate (a prescriber decision) or whether to pursue a formulary exception or PA.

### 19.3 Why Refills Stall on Coverage

A refill or new prescription commonly stalls because the insurer requires a PA, the medication is non-formulary, the plan changed at the start of a new year, or step therapy (trying a preferred medication first) is required. Staff identify the specific cause so the patient receives an accurate explanation and the correct next step, rather than a vague "insurance problem."

---

## 20. The Prior-Authorization Process, Step by Step

### 20.1 Step 1 — Identify the Requirement Early

When a medication is flagged as requiring PA — at the pharmacy, by the insurer's rejection, or by the prescriber's knowledge — staff begin the PA process **within 2 business days** of identifying it (Part 2, Section 9.4) and inform the patient that a coverage approval is needed and that the timeline depends partly on the insurer.

### 20.2 Step 2 — Gather Clinical Justification

The Prior Authorization Unit (585-230-2120), working with the prescriber, assembles the clinical information the insurer requires — for example, the diagnosis, prior medications tried and their outcomes, and relevant test results. Accurate, complete first submissions reduce delays and denials.

### 20.3 Step 3 — Submit and Track

The PA is submitted to the insurer through the required channel and tracked to a decision. Staff record the submission date and follow up proactively rather than waiting indefinitely. The patient is kept informed of status, particularly when the medication is essential and the patient may run short.

### 20.4 Step 4 — Protect Essential Therapy While Waiting

When the medication under PA is essential and the patient may run out before approval, staff coordinate interim protection: a bridge supply of a non-controlled essential medication (Part 2, Section 9.5), a covered alternative if the prescriber agrees, or, where appropriate, a manufacturer or pharmacy assistance option. For controlled substances, interim supply is a prescriber decision per Part 3. The patient is never simply left without an essential medication because a PA is pending.

### 20.5 Step 5 — Decision: Approval or Denial

On **approval**, the medication is dispensed or transmitted and the patient is notified. On **denial**, staff and the prescriber review the insurer's stated reason and decide among next steps: submit additional information, request a formulary exception, switch to a covered alternative the prescriber finds appropriate, or appeal (Section 20.6). The patient is told the outcome and the plan in plain language.

### 20.6 Step 6 — Appeals

When a PA is denied and the prescriber believes the medication is necessary, LRMC can pursue the insurer's appeal process, including a peer-to-peer review between the prescriber and the insurer's reviewer where available. The Prior Authorization Unit (585-230-2120) coordinates appeals and keeps the patient informed. Appeal timelines are set by the insurer.

### 20.7 Communication and Documentation

Throughout the PA process, the patient is kept informed of status and expected timing, and every step — identification, submission, insurer decision, interim protection, and appeals — is documented in the legal medical record per HIM-300. Because the insurer controls much of the timeline, staff set realistic expectations and avoid promising a date the insurer has not confirmed.

---

## 21. Cost, Coverage, and Assistance

### 21.1 Helping With High Out-of-Pocket Cost

When a medication is covered but expensive, staff can help the patient explore options: a covered lower-tier alternative the prescriber finds appropriate, manufacturer copay assistance for eligible patients, pharmacy or foundation assistance programs, and the LRMC financial-assistance resources coordinated with Patient Financial Services (585-230-7000). For specialty medications, the Specialty Pharmacy (585-230-2110) provides dedicated assistance (Part 6).

### 21.2 Keeping Clinical and Coverage Decisions Distinct

Staff are clear with patients about which barrier they face. A clinical decision (whether a medication is appropriate) belongs to the prescriber; a coverage decision (whether the insurer will pay) belongs to the insurer, with LRMC advocating through PA and appeals. Conflating the two confuses patients; separating them lets staff explain exactly what is happening and what comes next.

## Part 6 of 8 — Specialty, High-Cost, and Limited-Distribution Medications

## 22. What Specialty Medications Are and How They Differ

### 22.1 Definition and Examples

**Specialty medications** are high-cost, often injectable, infused, or biologic medications that typically require special handling (such as refrigeration), specific monitoring, manufacturer enrollment, or sourcing through a limited network of pharmacies. Examples include many medications for autoimmune and inflammatory disease, multiple sclerosis, hepatitis C, certain cancers, and other complex conditions. Specialty medications are coordinated by the **LRMC Specialty Pharmacy at 585-230-2110**, working with the prescriber and the Prior Authorization Unit (585-230-2120).

### 22.2 Why Specialty Refills Take Longer

Specialty refills involve more steps than ordinary refills: insurance prior authorization is almost always required; the medication may be available only through a specific limited-distribution pharmacy; enrollment in a manufacturer or hub program may be needed; financial-assistance arrangements are often part of getting the patient started; and cold-chain shipping must be scheduled. Because of these steps, specialty medications require more lead time, and patients are advised accordingly (Section 23.4 and Part 7).

---

## 23. The Specialty Refill Workflow

### 23.1 Coordination Hub

The Specialty Pharmacy (585-230-2110) serves as the coordination hub for specialty medications, connecting the patient, prescriber, insurer, manufacturer programs, and the dispensing pharmacy. A specialty refill request — whether from the portal, the patient, or the dispensing pharmacy — is routed to this hub rather than handled as an ordinary refill.

### 23.2 Benefits Investigation and Prior Authorization

For each specialty medication, staff perform a benefits investigation to determine coverage, the patient's expected cost, and the PA requirements, then pursue the PA through the Prior Authorization Unit (585-230-2120) under Part 5. Because specialty PAs are detailed, complete clinical documentation is assembled up front to reduce delays.

### 23.3 Financial Assistance and Enrollment

Specialty Pharmacy staff help eligible patients enroll in manufacturer copay assistance, foundation grants, and other programs that make high-cost medications affordable, and coordinate with Patient Financial Services (585-230-7000) as needed. Enrollment in a manufacturer hub or limited-distribution program is completed where the medication requires it.

### 23.4 Monitoring, Shipping, and Continuity

Many specialty medications require baseline and ongoing monitoring (for example, laboratory tests or screening before and during therapy). Staff confirm monitoring is current before each refill, schedule cold-chain shipment or in-clinic administration as applicable, and track the next due date so that continuity is maintained. Patients are advised to request specialty refills well ahead — generally **at least 2 weeks** before they will run out — because of the additional steps and shipping time.

### 23.5 Communication and Documentation

Specialty handling is communicated to the patient in plain language: what is needed, where the medication will come from, the expected cost and any assistance arranged, and the timing. Every step — benefits investigation, PA, enrollment, assistance, monitoring, and shipment — is documented in the legal medical record per HIM-300. Patient messages about specialty medications are reviewed and approved by qualified staff before release, consistent with PCT-100.

## 25. Tone and Safety Rules for All Refill Messages

Every refill message, regardless of template, follows these rules: write in plain, courteous language; tell the patient exactly what was done and what to do next; never imply suspicion when explaining a legal or safety rule; include safety-netting whenever a symptom or clinical concern could be present; offer interpreter support (Interpreter Services, 585-230-6400) when needed; and always provide the emergency instruction to call 911 for emergencies. A reply that denies or limits a request always states the reason in respectful, patient-appropriate terms and gives the patient a path forward.

## Part 8 of 8 — Common Patient Questions (FAQ), Safety Monitoring, and Appendices

## 26. Common Patient Questions

These questions are phrased the way patients ask them, with policy-grounded answers staff can adapt. They are written to be retrieved and used individually.

### 26.1 "How long does a refill take?"

Routine refills of non-controlled medications are processed within 2 business days of when we receive the request. If your prescription has no refills left, your provider has to review it, which can take up to 3 business days. If your medication needs lab work, a visit, or insurance approval first, it can take longer, and we will tell you what is needed. Please request routine refills at least 5 business days before you run out. If you have already run out of an essential medication, call the Outpatient Pharmacy at 585-230-2100 rather than waiting for a portal reply.

### 26.2 "Can I get my pain medication refilled through the portal?"

Pain medications that are controlled substances cannot be refilled by portal message. By law, many of these require a new prescription each time, and your provider must review the request, usually at an appointment. Please call Central Scheduling at 585-230-2600 to set up a visit, and we will make sure your provider reviews your request. This is a safety and legal requirement, not a reflection on you.

### 26.3 "Why do I need a lab test or appointment before my refill?"

Some medications need periodic checks — such as blood tests, a blood-pressure reading, or a follow-up visit — to make sure they are still working safely for you. When one of those is due, we ask you to complete it before refilling. If your medication is essential, we can give you a short supply to cover you until you do. Call us or Central Scheduling at 585-230-2600 to get it scheduled.

### 26.4 "My insurance won't cover my medication. What now?"

Sometimes an insurer requires prior approval (a prior authorization) or doesn't cover a medication on its formulary. We start the approval process within 2 business days of learning about it and keep you posted; the timing depends partly on your insurer. If approval is denied, your provider can consider an appeal or a covered alternative. If cost is the issue, we can look into assistance programs. Our Prior Authorization Unit can help at 585-230-2120.

### 26.5 "I lost my medication / it was damaged. Can I get more?"

For most regular (non-controlled) medications, we can arrange a replacement so you are not without an essential medication — just tell us your pharmacy and what happened, and be aware insurance may not cover an early replacement. For controlled medications, your provider has to review the situation and decide case by case, so please contact us and we will route it to them.

### 26.6 "I'm going on a trip and need extra medication. How do I arrange it?"

We can usually help you have enough for your trip. Please ask at least 2 weeks before you leave, and tell us your travel dates and destination. For regular medications we can often arrange a vacation supply, sometimes with an insurance override. Controlled medications have legal limits, so your provider must review those. Keep all medications in their original labeled containers when you travel.

### 26.7 "Can someone else pick up or request my refills?"

Someone can help with your medications if they are set up as an authorized proxy on your account, which protects your privacy. To add a proxy, contact the MyLRMC Help Desk at 585-230-3000. For controlled medications and sensitive medications, additional verification may apply.

### 26.8 "What's the fastest way to refill a regular medication?"

Use the MyLRMC portal or call our refill line at 585-230-2101, and request it at least 5 business days before you run out. Submitting through these channels routes your request straight into our review queue. If you've already run out of an essential medication, call the Outpatient Pharmacy at 585-230-2100 so we can expedite it.

### 26.9 "Can I get a 90-day supply by mail?"

Many stable, regular maintenance medications can be filled in 90-day supplies through mail order. We'll make sure enough refills are authorized so you don't have a gap, and remember to allow for shipping time. Let us know you'd like to use mail order and which pharmacy, and we'll help set it up.

### 26.10 "I take naloxone / was offered it. Why?"

If you take certain medications, including opioids, your care team may offer naloxone — a medication that can reverse an opioid overdose — as a safety precaution for you or those around you. It does not mean anyone thinks you are misusing your medication; it is a standard safety step. Your pharmacist can explain how to store and use it. The Outpatient Pharmacy can help at 585-230-2100.

## 27. Safety Monitoring and Quality Oversight

### 27.1 Turnaround and Backlog Monitoring

Clinic managers and pharmacy supervisors monitor refill-queue volumes and turnaround performance against the standards in Part 2, audit daily for aging or ownerless requests, and reassign work during absences so that no essential-medication request is delayed. Patterns of missed standards are reported to the Pharmacy and Therapeutics Committee.

### 27.2 Controlled-Substance Oversight

Controlled-substance prescribing and refill patterns are reviewed under CS-550, including registry-check compliance, adherence to treatment plans, and follow-up on lost/stolen reports and possible-diversion concerns through the Controlled-Substance Compliance / Diversion line (585-230-2150). Suspected staff diversion is reported to Compliance / Risk Management (585-230-8100).

### 27.3 Error and Near-Miss Reporting

Refill errors and near-misses — wrong medication, wrong dose, missed monitoring, delayed essential refills — are reported through the non-punitive safety-event system so the organization can learn and improve. Honest reporting is protected; concealment is not.

### 27.4 Oversight of the Automated Assistant

Use of the automated triage-support assistant in refill handling is monitored to confirm that the two standing rules hold: a qualified human reviews and approves every clinically significant medication reply before it is sent, and the assistant fails safe — it never auto-approves a medication and never lowers the handling a human would otherwise apply. Findings are reported to the Pharmacy and Therapeutics Committee and the Patient Communication Oversight Committee.

## Part 1 of 8 — Purpose, Scope, Governance, Definitions, and Access Philosophy

## 1. About This Manual

### 1.1 Title and Identification

This is the **Appointment Scheduling, Referrals & Access Policy Manual** of Lakeside Regional Medical Center (LRMC). It is policy document **SRA-250** within the LRMC Administrative Policy Library. It governs how LRMC schedules, reschedules, and cancels appointments; how it manages no-shows; how referrals to specialists are created and tracked; the wait-time standards the organization holds itself to; and the support services — transportation, interpretation, and disability accommodation — that make appointments accessible.

LRMC is a 400-bed community teaching hospital and affiliated outpatient network at 1200 Lakeshore Parkway, Rochester, NY 14600. The hospital main line is 585-230-0000. **Central Scheduling is 585-230-2600.** Appointment requests and scheduling questions also arrive through the **MyLRMC** patient portal, where the MyLRMC Help Desk is 585-230-3000.

### 1.2 Why This Manual Exists

Timely access to care is a safety and quality issue, not merely a customer-service one. A patient who cannot get a needed appointment, who is lost in a referral process, or who misses a visit because of a transportation or language barrier may go without care that matters. This manual exists to:

1. **Make access timely and equitable.** Define booking pathways and wait-time standards so patients are seen within clinically appropriate timeframes, and so barriers like language, disability, and transportation do not block care.
2. **Make scheduling consistent.** Give staff clear rules for booking, rescheduling, cancellation, and no-shows so patients are treated fairly and predictably across clinics.
3. **Close the referral loop.** Ensure referrals to specialists are created, communicated, scheduled, and tracked to completion, so patients do not fall through the cracks between providers.
4. **Support the front line.** Give scheduling and clinical staff concrete scripts and timelines for the scheduling questions patients ask most often.

### 1.3 How to Use This Manual

Each numbered section is written to stand on its own. If you need only the no-show rule, the new-patient booking timeline, or the steps to place a referral, you should be able to act from that single section without reading the entire manual. Cross-references point to related detail, but the essential rule is always restated where it is used.

Timeframes in this manual (for example, "established-patient routine visits are offered within 10 business days") are **policy standards**. A departure must be documented with the reason and managed under the access-monitoring rules in Part 5.

### 1.4 A Safety Note — Scheduling Is Not Triage

This manual governs **access and logistics**, not clinical triage. Scheduling staff do not assess symptoms or assign clinical urgency. When a scheduling message or call contains clinical content — a new or worsening symptom, a request that implies an emergency — it is handled first under the **Patient Communication & Triage Policy (PCT-100)** and the **Emergency, Urgent & After-Hours Care Guidelines (EUA-150)**, not booked as a routine appointment. A patient describing a possible emergency is directed to call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department; a patient describing an urgent symptom is routed to clinical staff or the after-hours nurse line (585-230-0911). Scheduling resumes only after clinical urgency is addressed.

### 1.5 Relationship to Other LRMC Policies

- **PCT-100 — Patient Communication and Triage Policy.** Governs how portal messages, including scheduling messages, are received and triaged; any clinical content is triaged first.
- **EUA-150 — Emergency, Urgent & After-Hours Care Guidelines.** Governs red-flag recognition and level-of-care guidance; a scheduling request that masks an emergency follows EUA-150.
- **PCT-210 — Telephone Triage and Nurse Advice Line Policy.** Governs spoken triage and the after-hours nurse line (585-230-0911).
- **PRIV-400 — Patient Privacy and Confidential Communications.** Governs identity verification, proxy access, and confidential-communication preferences for scheduling.
- **HIM-300 — Documentation and the Legal Medical Record.** Governs documentation of scheduling actions, referrals, and no-shows that are clinically significant.
- **RX-500 — Outpatient Prescription and Refill Management.** Referenced when a visit is required before a medication can be renewed (Pharmacy, 585-230-2100).
- **FIN-700 — Patient Financial Services and Coverage.** Governs insurance, authorization for referrals, and cost questions (Patient Financial Services, 585-230-7000).

## 2. Scope and Applicability

### 2.1 What This Manual Covers

This manual applies to **outpatient appointment scheduling, referral management, and access support** across LRMC's affiliated clinics and outpatient network, including:

- Booking, rescheduling, and canceling outpatient appointments through Central Scheduling (585-230-2600), the MyLRMC portal, and clinic front desks.
- New-patient and established-patient visit timelines and visit types (in-person and telehealth).
- No-show and late-cancellation handling.
- Referrals from primary care to specialists and the specialist-access workflow.
- Wait-time standards and access monitoring.
- Transportation assistance, interpreter services (585-230-6400), and disability accommodations.
- Responding to patient scheduling messages.

### 2.2 What This Manual Does Not Cover

This manual does **not** govern: clinical triage or symptom assessment (PCT-100, EUA-150); Emergency Department registration and care; inpatient admissions and bed management; surgical scheduling beyond the outpatient referral and pre-visit steps (governed by the perioperative services policy); detailed insurance and billing rules (FIN-700); or prescription refills (RX-500), except where a visit is a prerequisite to a refill.

### 2.3 Who Must Follow This Manual

This manual applies to every LRMC workforce member who schedules, reschedules, cancels, or supports access to appointments, or who creates or tracks referrals, including: Central Scheduling staff; clinic front-desk and Patient Access staff; referral coordinators; medical assistants and clinical support staff who arrange follow-up; nurses and providers who order referrals or direct scheduling; and supervisors and managers who monitor access. Only staff who have completed the required training (Part 8) perform these functions.

## 3. Access Philosophy

### 3.1 Timely Access Is Part of Safe Care

LRMC treats timely access as a component of clinical quality. The right visit at the right time prevents deterioration, supports chronic-disease management, and catches problems early. Wait-time standards (Part 5) exist to make "timely" concrete and measurable, and access is monitored so that gaps are found and fixed rather than tolerated.

### 3.2 Match Urgency to Timeframe

Appointments are offered within a timeframe appropriate to the **clinical urgency** of the need, as determined by clinical staff — not by who calls most persistently. Urgent needs get same-day or next-day options; routine needs are scheduled within routine standards. Because scheduling staff do not assess urgency, they rely on clinician direction and on the triage process in PCT-100 to set the urgency of a visit.

### 3.3 Equity and Access for All Patients

Access support is part of access. Language, disability, transportation, and digital barriers should not determine whether a patient receives care. Interpreter services (585-230-6400), disability accommodations, transportation assistance, and non-portal scheduling options (phone, front desk) are offered so that every patient can reach and complete their appointments.

### 3.4 Close Every Loop

A scheduling action is complete when the patient has a confirmed plan they understand — an appointment booked, a referral placed and being scheduled, or a clear next step — and any required follow-up is owned by someone. Referrals, in particular, are tracked to completion (Part 4); a referral that is ordered but never scheduled is a failure of the loop, not an acceptable outcome.

### 3.5 Respect, Clarity, and Documentation

Scheduling communication is plain, respectful, and specific — dates, times, locations, what to bring, and how to change a visit. Clinically significant scheduling actions (such as a referral or a pattern of missed visits affecting care) are documented per HIM-300. Patient privacy is protected under PRIV-400, including verifying identity and honoring confidential-communication preferences.

## 5. Definitions

Each definition stands alone so a reader can resolve it wherever the term appears.

**New patient.** A patient who has not been seen by the requested provider or specialty within LRMC in the past three years, or who has no established relationship with that provider/specialty. New-patient visits typically require more time and additional registration.

**Established patient.** A patient with an existing relationship with the requested provider or specialty, seen within the past three years. Established-patient visits are generally scheduled within shorter timeframes.

**Routine visit.** A non-urgent appointment for ongoing or preventive care — for example, a follow-up, an annual wellness visit, or a stable chronic-condition check.

**Urgent visit.** A non-emergency appointment needed promptly (typically same-day or next-day) for a problem that should not wait for a routine slot, as determined by clinical staff. Urgent **clinical** assessment of whether something is urgent is done under PCT-100/EUA-150, not by scheduling staff.

**Telehealth visit.** A scheduled video or phone visit with a clinician (LRMC Virtual Care). Appropriate for needs that do not require a physical exam or in-person testing; not for emergencies.

**Referral.** A request from one clinician (usually primary care) for a patient to be seen by a specialist or service, including the clinical reason and any supporting information. Referrals are created, communicated, scheduled, and tracked under Part 4.

**Specialist access / specialty appointment.** An appointment with a specialty service to which a patient has been referred. Access timeframes depend on specialty and urgency (Part 4, Part 5).

**Prior authorization (referral).** Approval an insurer may require before it will cover a specialist visit or service. Coordinated with Patient Financial Services (585-230-7000) under FIN-700.

**No-show.** A scheduled appointment the patient neither attended nor canceled in advance, as defined in Part 3.

**Late cancellation.** A cancellation made fewer than the required number of hours before the appointment, as defined in Part 3.

**Wait time / third-next-available.** A standard measure of access — the number of days until the third-next-available appointment of a given type — used because it reflects true availability better than the very next open slot. Wait-time standards are in Part 5.

**Proxy.** An individual authorized under PRIV-400 to schedule and communicate on a patient's behalf, such as a parent of a minor or caregiver of a dependent adult. Proxy status is verified before scheduling or sharing information.

## Part 2 of 8 — Booking Appointments: New vs. Established Patients, Visit Types, and Timelines

## 6. Booking Channels

Patients book appointments through three channels, all feeding the same scheduling system:

1. **MyLRMC patient portal** — self-scheduling for eligible visit types and appointment requests for others. Available 24/7 for requests; staffed review during business hours.
2. **Central Scheduling (585-230-2600)** — staffed by phone during business hours for all visit types, new and established patients, and complex bookings.
3. **Clinic front desk** — in-person booking and follow-up scheduling at the end of a visit.

All channels verify patient identity or proxy authority under PRIV-400 before booking or sharing appointment details, and all route any clinical content to triage under PCT-100 before booking (Section 1.4).

---

## 7. New-Patient Booking

### 7.1 Who Is a New Patient

A **new patient** is one who has not been seen by the requested provider or specialty within LRMC in the past three years, or who has no established relationship with that provider or specialty. New-patient visits are typically longer and require registration and records steps before the visit.

### 7.2 New-Patient Booking Steps

1. **Verify identity and create/confirm the record** (PRIV-400), including demographics and insurance.
2. **Confirm the visit type and reason** at a non-clinical level (for example, "establish primary care," "new specialist consult"); clinical content is triaged first under PCT-100.
3. **Confirm insurance and any referral/authorization** needed (FIN-700; Patient Financial Services 585-230-7000), especially for specialty visits (Part 4).
4. **Request prior records** where relevant so the provider has them at the visit (Health Information Management 585-230-4200).
5. **Book within the new-patient timeline** (Section 7.3) and send confirmation with date, time, location, what to bring, and how to reschedule.

### 7.3 New-Patient Timelines

- **New primary-care patient (routine establish-care visit):** offered within **15 business days**.
- **New specialty consult (routine):** offered within the specialty's routine standard, generally **within 4 weeks**, sooner when the referral is marked urgent (Part 4, Part 5).
- **Urgent new-patient need** (as determined by clinical staff): offered same-day or next-day options, or directed to urgent care/ED per EUA-150 if the need exceeds what a scheduled visit can safely cover.

### 7.4 New-Patient Preparation

New patients are told what to bring (identification, insurance, medication list, relevant records), how to complete pre-visit registration through MyLRMC or by phone, and how to request an interpreter (585-230-6400), transportation assistance (585-230-2800), or a disability accommodation (585-230-6450). Completing registration in advance shortens the visit and reduces delays.

---

## 8. Established-Patient Booking

### 8.1 Who Is an Established Patient

An **established patient** has an existing relationship with the requested provider or specialty, seen within the past three years. Established visits are generally booked more quickly because the record and relationship already exist.

### 8.2 Established-Patient Timelines

- **Routine established visit** (follow-up, stable chronic-condition check): offered within **10 business days**.
- **Preventive/wellness visit:** offered within **20 business days**, scheduled to align with recommended timing.
- **Urgent established need** (clinically determined): same-day or next-day options; if it exceeds a scheduled visit's safe scope, directed to urgent care or the ED per EUA-150.
- **Post-hospitalization follow-up:** offered within **7 calendar days** of discharge (or sooner if clinically directed), because timely follow-up after a hospital stay reduces complications and readmissions.

### 8.3 Self-Scheduling for Established Patients

Eligible established patients may self-schedule certain visit types through MyLRMC (for example, routine follow-ups and wellness visits with their own provider). Self-scheduling is limited to visit types where booking without staff review is safe; anything requiring clinical judgment, a referral, or special preparation is requested through the portal and reviewed by staff.

---

## 9. Visit Types and Choosing Between Them

### 9.1 In-Person Visits

In-person visits are scheduled when a physical examination, in-person testing, a procedure, or hands-on care is needed, and whenever the patient prefers in person. New-patient establish-care visits are commonly in person.

### 9.2 Telehealth Visits

Telehealth visits (LRMC Virtual Care, 585-230-3500) are scheduled for needs that can be safely handled remotely — many follow-ups, medication management, behavioral-health visits without a crisis, and review of stable results — when the patient has the means and prefers or accepts a virtual visit. Telehealth is **not** for emergencies; a telehealth request that reveals a red flag is redirected per EUA-150. Patients are told what they need for a video visit and offered help if they lack the technology or need a phone visit instead.

### 9.3 Same-Day and Urgent Slots

Each primary-care clinic holds a portion of its daily schedule for same-day and urgent needs, so that clinically urgent problems can be seen promptly without displacing routine care entirely. Scheduling staff offer these slots based on clinical direction (PCT-100), not on patient insistence alone.

### 9.4 Matching the Patient to the Right Provider

Bookings honor continuity — scheduling the patient with their own provider or care team when possible — while balancing timeliness. When the patient's own provider is not available within the needed timeframe, the patient is offered a same-team or covering provider, or a telehealth option, rather than being made to wait past the clinically appropriate window.

---

## 10. Confirmations and Reminders

### 10.1 Confirmation at Booking

At booking, the patient receives a confirmation (in the portal, by message, or verbally) stating the date, time, provider, location or telehealth link, visit reason at a non-clinical level, what to bring or prepare, and how to reschedule or cancel. Confirmations honor the patient's communication preferences under PRIV-400.

### 10.2 Reminders

LRMC sends appointment reminders in advance through the patient's chosen channel — typically a reminder several days before and again the day before. Reminders include how to confirm, reschedule, or cancel, and a prompt to request an interpreter, transportation, or accommodation if needed. Reminders reduce no-shows and give patients a clear, easy path to change a visit they cannot keep.

## Part 3 of 8 — Rescheduling, Cancellation, and No-Show Policy

## 11. Rescheduling

### 11.1 How Patients Reschedule

Patients may reschedule through MyLRMC, by calling Central Scheduling (585-230-2600), or at a clinic front desk. LRMC encourages patients to reschedule as early as possible so the released slot can be offered to another patient. There is **no penalty for rescheduling in advance** within the cancellation window (Section 12); rescheduling is the preferred alternative to a no-show.

### 11.2 Rescheduling Within Clinical Timeframes

When a patient reschedules, staff offer a new appointment within the same clinically appropriate timeframe as the original. A patient rescheduling an urgent or post-hospitalization visit is not pushed to a distant routine slot; the clinical timeframe still governs. If a patient repeatedly reschedules a clinically important visit, staff note the pattern and, where care could be affected, involve the care team.

### 11.3 LRMC-Initiated Rescheduling

When LRMC must reschedule (for example, provider absence), staff contact the patient promptly, apologize for the change, and offer the earliest appropriate alternative, prioritizing patients whose needs are time-sensitive. LRMC-initiated reschedules are never counted against the patient.

---

## 12. Cancellation

### 12.1 Cancellation Window

LRMC asks patients to cancel at least **24 hours** before a scheduled appointment (and at least **48 hours** before certain longer or procedural visits, where noted at booking) so the slot can be reused. Cancellations made within this window are routine and carry no penalty.

### 12.2 How to Cancel

Patients cancel through MyLRMC, by calling Central Scheduling (585-230-2600), or at the front desk. The cancellation is confirmed to the patient, and, for a clinically important visit, the patient is offered rescheduling at the same time so the need is not dropped.

### 12.3 Late Cancellation

A **late cancellation** is one made fewer than the required hours before the appointment (fewer than 24 hours for standard visits, or fewer than 48 hours for designated longer/procedural visits). Late cancellations are recorded. Because they leave little time to offer the slot to another patient, repeated late cancellations are handled like repeated no-shows for outreach purposes (Section 13), with attention to barriers the patient may be facing.

---

## 13. No-Show Policy

### 13.1 Definition

A **no-show** is a scheduled appointment the patient neither attended nor canceled in advance. No-shows are recorded in the record. A single no-show is treated as a possible sign of a barrier (transportation, language, confusion about the date, illness, or cost), not as misconduct.

### 13.2 Response to a No-Show

After a no-show, staff:

1. **Reach out** to the patient through their preferred channel to reschedule, and ask whether a barrier prevented the visit.
2. **Address barriers** — offer transportation assistance (585-230-2800), interpreter services (585-230-6400), accommodation (585-230-6450), telehealth, or a more convenient time.
3. **Protect clinically important care** — for a missed visit that matters clinically (for example, a post-hospitalization follow-up, an urgent referral, or monitoring tied to a medication under RX-500), notify the care team so the need is not lost.
4. **Document** the no-show and the outreach per HIM-300.

### 13.3 Repeated No-Shows

When a patient has repeated no-shows, staff make additional outreach attempts, work to identify and resolve barriers, and involve the care team and, where appropriate, social-work or patient-navigation resources. LRMC's emphasis is on **re-engaging** the patient. Any administrative consequence for chronic missed appointments follows a defined, fair process with patient notice, applies only after barrier-resolution efforts, and **never** applies to urgent or emergency care — a patient is never turned away from emergency care because of a no-show history. Such measures are reviewed under PASC oversight and documented.

### 13.4 No-Shows and Vulnerable Patients

Extra care is taken with patients who may face higher barriers — for example, older adults, patients with disabilities, patients with limited English proficiency, and patients with behavioral-health needs. For these patients, repeated missed visits trigger supportive outreach and barrier resolution rather than penalty, and behavioral-health no-shows for at-risk patients are flagged to the behavioral-health team (585-230-3911).

---

## 14. Waitlists and Earlier-Appointment Options

### 14.1 Waitlist for Earlier Slots

When the soonest appropriate appointment is later than a patient would like, staff offer to add the patient to a waitlist for an earlier slot if one opens (for example, from a cancellation). The patient is told how the waitlist works and that the original appointment is held unless an earlier one is confirmed.

### 14.2 Offering Released Slots

Slots released by cancellations and reschedules are offered first to patients with the most time-sensitive needs and to those on the waitlist. This recycling of slots is a key way LRMC shortens effective wait times (Part 5).

## Part 4 of 8 — Referrals and Specialist-Access Workflow

## 15. The Referral Principle — Close the Loop

A **referral** sends a patient from one clinician (usually primary care) to a specialist or service. The defining standard at LRMC is that **every referral is tracked to completion**: created with a clear reason, communicated to the patient and the specialty, scheduled within an appropriate timeframe, and followed until the patient is seen and the result returns to the referring clinician. A referral that is ordered but never scheduled, or a specialist note that never returns to the referring provider, is a broken loop and a patient-safety risk. The Referral Management Office (585-230-2650) owns referral tracking.

---

## 16. Creating a Referral

### 16.1 Step 1 — Clinical Order and Reason

The referring clinician creates the referral with the specialty or service requested, the clinical reason, the **urgency** (routine, urgent, or — rarely, for a time-critical finding — emergent, which is handled as an emergency under EUA-150, not a routine referral), and any supporting information (relevant history, results, images). The urgency set by the clinician drives the scheduling timeframe (Section 18).

### 16.2 Step 2 — Insurance and Authorization

The Referral Management Office and Patient Financial Services (585-230-7000) determine whether the patient's insurance requires a referral authorization or prior authorization for the specialty visit (FIN-700) and obtain it. Authorization is pursued promptly so it does not delay scheduling; the patient is informed if their plan requires it and what it means.

### 16.3 Step 3 — Patient Communication

The patient is told that a referral has been placed, to which specialty and why (at an appropriate level), how and when they will be scheduled, and whom to contact with questions (Referral Management Office, 585-230-2650). The patient is given the choice of an LRMC specialist or, where applicable, an external in-network specialist, and is offered help with any barrier (interpreter, transportation, accommodation).

### 16.4 Step 4 — Records Transfer

Relevant records, results, and images are made available to the receiving specialist so the consult is informed and not delayed by missing information (Health Information Management, 585-230-4200), consistent with PRIV-400.

---

## 17. Scheduling and Tracking the Specialist Visit

### 17.1 Scheduling the Specialty Appointment

The specialty appointment is scheduled within the timeframe matching the referral's urgency (Section 18). For an internal LRMC specialty, scheduling is coordinated directly; for an external specialist, the Referral Management Office helps the patient book and confirms the appointment is made. The patient is not left to navigate the process alone.

### 17.2 Tracking to Completion

The Referral Management Office tracks each referral through these states and does not close it until the loop is complete:

1. **Placed** — referral created and, if needed, authorized.
2. **Scheduled** — specialty appointment booked within the appropriate timeframe.
3. **Seen** — the patient attended the specialty visit (or a no-show is detected and outreach begun under Part 3).
4. **Result returned** — the specialist's findings and recommendations are communicated back to the referring clinician.
5. **Closed** — the referring clinician has the result and any follow-up is arranged.

### 17.3 Catching Broken Loops

Referrals that stall — not scheduled within the expected window, a no-show with no follow-up, or no specialist result returned — are flagged for the Referral Management Office to act on: re-contacting the patient, rebooking, chasing authorization, or obtaining the missing specialist report. Stalled urgent referrals are escalated promptly because delay can cause harm. The PASC monitors referral-completion metrics (Part 5).

---

## 18. Specialist-Access Timeframes by Urgency

The referral's clinically assigned urgency sets the target time to the specialty visit:

- **Routine referral:** specialty visit offered within **4 weeks** (sooner for specialties with shorter standards).
- **Urgent referral:** specialty visit offered within **5 business days**, or sooner per clinical direction.
- **Emergent finding:** not handled as a scheduled referral — the patient is directed to emergency care under EUA-150 (call 911 or go to the ED), and the specialty is engaged urgently through clinical channels.

When a specialty's capacity cannot meet the target, the Referral Management Office offers the earliest available alternative (including another qualified specialist or an external in-network option), informs the referring clinician, and escalates capacity gaps to the PASC.

---

## 19. Self-Referral and Direct-Access Services

Some services may be accessed without a primary-care referral, depending on the service and the patient's insurance — for example, certain behavioral-health, women's-health, eye, or preventive services. Where direct access is available, staff help the patient book directly and confirm whether their insurance still requires any authorization (FIN-700). When a referral **is** required by the plan or the service, staff explain this and route the request to the patient's primary-care team to place the referral.

## Part 5 of 8 — Wait-Time Standards, Access Monitoring, and Capacity Management

## 20. Wait-Time Standards

LRMC holds itself to the following access standards, measured and monitored continuously. These are **policy standards**; gaps are managed under Sections 21–22, not ignored.

### 20.1 Primary-Care Access Standards

- **Urgent (clinically determined):** same-day or next-day.
- **Routine established visit:** within **10 business days**.
- **New patient (establish care):** within **15 business days**.
- **Preventive/wellness:** within **20 business days**.
- **Post-hospitalization follow-up:** within **7 calendar days** of discharge.

### 20.2 Specialty-Care Access Standards

- **Urgent referral:** within **5 business days**.
- **Routine referral:** within **4 weeks** (sooner where a specialty sets a shorter standard).

### 20.3 Telehealth and Behavioral Health

- **Telehealth visits** are scheduled within the same standards as the equivalent in-person visit type.
- **Behavioral-health access** follows BH-600; urgent behavioral-health needs and crises are handled immediately (Behavioral Health Access Line, 585-230-3911; and EUA-150 for crisis), not placed in a routine queue.

### 20.4 How Wait Time Is Measured

LRMC measures access primarily by **third-next-available appointment** for each visit type and provider, because the third-next-available reflects real, repeatable availability better than a single open slot that may be an anomaly. Wait times are tracked by clinic, provider, and specialty, and reviewed by the PASC.

---

## 21. Access Monitoring

### 21.1 Ongoing Metrics

The PASC reviews access metrics on an ongoing basis, including: third-next-available by visit type and specialty; new-patient and post-hospitalization timeliness; referral-completion rates and time-to-specialty-visit; no-show and late-cancellation rates; portal vs. phone booking mix; and interpreter, transportation, and accommodation utilization. Metrics are stratified to detect inequities in access across patient groups.

### 21.2 Acting on Gaps

When a metric falls short of standard — for example, a specialty's wait time exceeds the routine standard — the PASC investigates the cause (capacity, scheduling-template, no-show, or demand) and acts: adjusting templates, adding same-day capacity, recruiting or redistributing capacity, addressing no-show drivers, or expanding telehealth. Patients affected by a capacity gap are offered the earliest appropriate alternative and, for time-sensitive needs, are actively rebooked rather than left waiting.

### 21.3 When a Standard Cannot Be Met for a Patient

If an individual patient cannot be offered an appointment within the applicable standard, staff offer the earliest available appropriate alternative (including a covering provider, another qualified specialist, telehealth, or an external in-network option), document the gap, and — for an urgent or clinically time-sensitive need — escalate so the patient is seen safely. A patient with an urgent need is never simply told to wait past a safe window; their need is escalated to clinical staff and, if it exceeds what a scheduled visit can safely cover, redirected per EUA-150.

---

## 22. Capacity Management

### 22.1 Schedule Templates and Same-Day Capacity

Clinics design schedule templates to balance pre-booked routine care with protected same-day/urgent capacity, so that urgent needs are met without crowding out routine access. Templates are reviewed against demand and adjusted by the PASC and clinic managers.

### 22.2 Surge and Backlog

When demand surges or a backlog develops (for example, after a provider absence or a seasonal spike), supervisors prioritize urgent and time-sensitive visits, recycle released slots to waitlisted and high-need patients, expand telehealth and covering-provider capacity, and escalate persistent gaps to the PASC. Backlogs affecting urgent access are escalated immediately.

### 22.3 Provider Absence Coverage

When a provider is absent, their time-sensitive appointments are covered by same-team or covering providers or telehealth, and affected patients are contacted and rebooked within the appropriate clinical timeframe. Coverage is arranged before the absence wherever possible.

## Part 6 of 8 — Transportation, Interpreter, and Accommodation Services

## 23. Access Support Is Part of Access

An appointment a patient cannot reach, understand, or physically attend is not real access. LRMC offers transportation assistance, interpreter and language services, and disability accommodations so that practical barriers do not prevent care. These services are offered proactively — at booking, in reminders, and after a no-show — not only when a patient happens to ask.

---

## 24. Interpreter and Language Services

### 24.1 Right to an Interpreter

Patients with limited English proficiency and patients who are deaf or hard of hearing have the right to a qualified interpreter at no cost to them. LRMC provides interpretation for scheduling and for visits through **Interpreter & Language Services (585-230-6400)**, including spoken-language interpreters and sign-language interpreters, in person or by video/phone as appropriate.

### 24.2 Arranging Interpretation

Patients' language needs are recorded in the record and honored automatically for future contacts under PRIV-400. At booking, staff confirm whether an interpreter is needed and arrange it for the visit; reminders prompt patients to request interpretation if their needs change. Staff do **not** rely on family members or minors to interpret clinical information; a qualified interpreter is used.

### 24.3 Translated and Accessible Materials

Scheduling confirmations, instructions, and patient materials are made available in the patient's language and in accessible formats where needed. Patients are told how to request materials in another language or format.

---

## 25. Transportation Assistance

### 25.1 What Is Available

For patients who lack reliable transportation, LRMC's **Transportation Assistance / Patient Mobility office (585-230-2800)** helps arrange or coordinate transportation to appointments, including information on covered non-emergency medical transportation where the patient's insurance provides it, community transportation resources, and accessible-vehicle options for patients with mobility needs.

### 25.2 Arranging Transportation

Transportation needs are identified at booking, in reminders, and especially after a transportation-related no-show. Staff help the patient set up transportation in advance of clinically important visits (for example, post-hospitalization follow-up or an urgent referral) so a ride does not become the reason a needed visit is missed. Transportation help is coordinated, not assumed; the patient confirms the plan.

### 25.3 Transportation and No-Shows

When a no-show appears transportation-related, the response is to solve the transportation problem and rebook, not to penalize the patient (Part 3). Transportation barriers are a leading, addressable cause of missed care.

---

## 26. Disability Accommodations

### 26.1 Accommodations Offered

LRMC provides reasonable accommodations so that patients with disabilities can access appointments, coordinated through **Disability Access & Accommodations (585-230-6450)**. Examples include accessible exam rooms and equipment, sign-language interpretation (with Language Services), materials in accessible formats, additional visit time where needed, support for service animals, and assistance navigating the facility.

### 26.2 Arranging Accommodations

Accommodation needs are recorded and honored on future contacts under PRIV-400. At booking, staff ask whether an accommodation is needed and arrange it in advance, and reminders prompt patients to request accommodations. Patients are told whom to contact (585-230-6450) if a need arises or is not met.

### 26.3 Telehealth Accessibility and the Digital Divide

Telehealth is offered with attention to accessibility — captioning, screen-reader-compatible materials, and a phone-visit option for patients without video capability — so that virtual care does not exclude patients who lack technology or have disabilities. Patients who cannot use the portal are fully supported through phone scheduling (585-230-2600) and the front desk; the portal is never the only way to access care.

## Part 7 of 8 — Responding to Scheduling Messages: Scripts and Templates

## 27. How to Use These Scripts

The templates below are starting points for the scheduling messages patients send most often. They are plain, respectful, and specific. Two rules govern every one of them, restated here because each may be read alone:

1. **Triage clinical content first.** If a scheduling message contains any symptom or clinical concern, it is handled under PCT-100/EUA-150 before booking — a possible emergency is directed to 911/ED, and an urgent symptom is routed to clinical staff or the after-hours nurse line (585-230-0911). Scheduling staff do not assess symptoms.
2. **Verify identity/proxy** under PRIV-400 before sharing or changing appointment details.

Square brackets mark fields to complete. A qualified human reviews any reply that touches clinical content before it is sent.

### 27.1 "How do I book an appointment?"

> [Patient first name], we'd be glad to help you book. You can schedule through the MyLRMC portal, or call Central Scheduling at 585-230-2600 (Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m.–5:00 p.m.). Please let us know the reason for the visit and your preferred days/times, and whether you'd like in-person or a video visit. If you need an interpreter, transportation, or any accommodation, tell us and we'll arrange it. If you have a new or worsening symptom, let us know so the right team can help — and for a medical emergency, call 911.

### 27.2 "How do I reschedule or cancel?"

> [Patient first name], you can reschedule or cancel through the MyLRMC portal, by calling Central Scheduling at 585-230-2600, or at the front desk. Please give us at least 24 hours' notice (48 hours for longer or procedure visits) so we can offer the time to someone else. Would you like me to find you a new time now? If this visit is for an urgent concern, let us know so we keep it within the right timeframe. For a medical emergency, call 911.

### 27.3 "When is the next available appointment?"

> [Patient first name], for [visit type] with [provider/specialty], the next available is [date/time], and I can also add you to our waitlist in case an earlier slot opens. If you'd like to be seen sooner for an urgent concern, tell us what's going on so the right team can help decide the timing. Would you like me to book [date/time] or look at other options? For a medical emergency, call 911.

### 27.4 "I was referred to a specialist — what now?"

> [Patient first name], your provider has placed a referral to [specialty]. Our Referral Management Office (585-230-2650) will help get you scheduled and handle any insurance approval. You should be offered an appointment within [timeframe based on urgency]. You can choose an LRMC specialist or an in-network specialist near you. Let us know if you need an interpreter, transportation, or an accommodation. If your symptoms worsen while you wait, contact us — and for a medical emergency, call 911.

### 27.5 "I missed my appointment."

> [Patient first name], no problem — let's get you rescheduled. Was there anything that made it hard to get there, like transportation, timing, or needing an interpreter? We can help with those. I can offer [date/time] or find another time that works better. If this visit was for something time-sensitive, we'll make sure it's not delayed. For a medical emergency, call 911.

### 27.6 "Can I have a video visit instead?"

> [Patient first name], many visits can be done by video through LRMC Virtual Care (585-230-3500). I can check whether [visit type] is suitable for telehealth and switch it if so. You'll need [device/internet], and if video won't work for you, we can do a phone visit instead. Would you like me to set that up? Note that video visits aren't for emergencies — for those, call 911.

### 27.7 "I need an interpreter / transportation / an accommodation."

> [Patient first name], we'll take care of that. I've noted that you need [a [language] interpreter / transportation help / [accommodation]] and will arrange it for your visit, at no cost to you. For future visits we'll have this on file. You can also reach Interpreter & Language Services at 585-230-6400, Transportation Assistance at 585-230-2800, or Disability Access at 585-230-6450. Is there anything else you need to make your visit easier?

### 27.8 "I need to be seen for a new problem."

> [Patient first name], thank you for letting us know. So we get you the right kind of visit at the right time, tell us a bit about what's going on. [If any symptom is present: I'm routing this to our clinical team to review. / A nurse will follow up.] If your symptoms are severe or getting worse — for example, trouble breathing, chest pain, or signs of a stroke — call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department now. For an urgent concern after hours, call our nurse line at 585-230-0911.

---

## 28. Tone and Safety Rules for Scheduling Messages

Every scheduling reply: confirms or offers specific dates/times and the channel to act; verifies identity/proxy before sharing details (PRIV-400); offers interpreter (585-230-6400), transportation (585-230-2800), and accommodation (585-230-6450) support where relevant; routes any clinical content to triage first (PCT-100/EUA-150); and includes the standing instruction to call 911 for an emergency whenever a health concern could be present. A reply that cannot fully resolve the request gives a clear next step, owner, and timeframe.

## Part 8 of 8 — Common Patient Questions (FAQ), Quality Monitoring, and Appendices

## 29. Common Patient Questions

These are phrased the way patients ask them, with policy-grounded answers staff can adapt. Each stands alone for retrieval.

### 29.1 "How soon can I get an appointment?"

It depends on the type of visit. Urgent problems are seen the same or next day. Routine visits with your own provider are offered within about 10 business days, new-patient visits within about 15 business days, and wellness visits within about 20 business days. After a hospital stay, we aim to see you within 7 days. If you need to be seen sooner for an urgent concern, tell us what's going on so we can arrange the right timing — and for an emergency, call 911.

### 29.2 "How do I book or reschedule online?"

Log into the MyLRMC portal to request or, for many visit types, directly book and reschedule appointments. If you don't see the option you need, send an appointment request through the portal or call Central Scheduling at 585-230-2600 and we'll handle it. Need portal help? Contact the MyLRMC Help Desk at 585-230-3000.

### 29.3 "What is your cancellation and no-show policy?"

Please cancel at least 24 hours ahead (48 hours for longer or procedure visits) so we can offer the time to another patient — there's no penalty for canceling in advance. If you miss a visit, just reach out and we'll reschedule; we'll also ask if something like transportation or timing got in the way so we can help. We never withhold emergency or urgent care because of missed appointments.

### 29.4 "Do I need a referral to see a specialist?"

It depends on your insurance and the specialty. Some plans require a referral from your primary-care provider, and some specialties can be booked directly. Tell us which specialty you need and we'll check. If a referral is required, your primary-care team will place it, and our Referral Management Office (585-230-2650) will help you get scheduled and handle any insurance approval.

### 29.5 "I have a referral but no one has scheduled me — what do I do?"

Let's fix that. Our Referral Management Office (585-230-2650) tracks referrals until you're seen, so contact us and we'll find your referral and get you scheduled within the right timeframe. If your symptoms have worsened while waiting, tell us so we can reassess the urgency — and for an emergency, call 911.

### 29.6 "Can I see a doctor by video?"

Yes, many visits can be done by video through LRMC Virtual Care (585-230-3500) — follow-ups, medication management, behavioral-health visits, and review of stable results. Some visits need an in-person exam or testing, and we'll tell you if yours does. If you don't have video, we can often do a phone visit. Video isn't for emergencies — for those, call 911.

### 29.7 "Can you help me get a ride to my appointment?"

Yes. Our Transportation Assistance office (585-230-2800) can help you arrange transportation, including options your insurance may cover and community resources. Let us know before your visit, especially for important appointments, and we'll help set it up so a ride isn't the reason you miss care.

### 29.8 "I need an interpreter — is that available?"

Absolutely, at no cost to you. We provide interpreters in many spoken languages and sign-language interpreters, in person or by video/phone. Tell us your language and we'll arrange it for your visit and keep it on file for next time. You can also reach Interpreter & Language Services at 585-230-6400.

### 29.9 "Can my family member schedule for me?"

Yes, if they're set up as an authorized proxy on your account, which protects your privacy. To add a proxy, contact the MyLRMC Help Desk at 585-230-3000. We verify proxy authority before scheduling or sharing your appointment details.

### 29.10 "What if I can't be seen as soon as I need?"

If we can't offer a slot within the usual timeframe, we'll find you the earliest appropriate option — a covering provider, another qualified specialist, a video visit, or an in-network option nearby — and we'll add you to a waitlist for earlier openings. If your need is urgent, tell us, because we escalate urgent needs rather than letting you wait. For an emergency, call 911.

## 30. Quality Monitoring and Oversight

### 30.1 Access and Referral Audits

Supervisors and the PASC audit access and referral performance: third-next-available by visit type and specialty, new-patient and post-hospitalization timeliness, referral-completion rates and stalled-referral counts, no-show and late-cancellation rates, and utilization of interpreter, transportation, and accommodation services. Stalled urgent referrals and access gaps for time-sensitive needs are reviewed promptly.

### 30.2 Equity Monitoring

Access metrics are stratified to detect disparities — for example, differences in wait times, no-show rates, or referral completion across patient groups — so that inequities are identified and addressed. Barrier-resolution efforts (transportation, language, accommodation) are tracked as part of equity oversight.

### 30.3 Safety-Event Reporting

Access failures that affect care — a missed urgent referral, a clinically important visit lost to an unaddressed barrier, a scheduling error that delayed needed care — are reported through the non-punitive safety-event system and reviewed by the PASC, with serious events escalated to Compliance / Risk Management (585-230-8100). Reviews drive process improvement, not blame.

### 30.4 Patient Feedback

Patient experience with access — ease of booking, wait times, referral navigation, and support services — is gathered and reviewed by the PASC and Patient Relations (585-230-5500), and used to improve scheduling pathways and reduce barriers.

---

## 31. Training Requirements

No staff member schedules, manages referrals, or handles access support until they complete training covering: booking pathways and new-vs-established timelines (Part 2); rescheduling, cancellation, and no-show handling with a barrier-first, non-punitive approach (Part 3); the referral workflow and loop-closure tracking (Part 4); wait-time standards and escalation of gaps (Part 5); transportation, interpreter, and accommodation services (Part 6); scheduling scripts and the rule to triage clinical content first (Part 7); and identity/proxy verification under PRIV-400. Staff are trained to recognize when a scheduling message contains clinical content and to route it to triage (PCT-100/EUA-150) rather than book it. Refresher training is completed periodically and after significant changes.

## 32. Appendices

### 32.1 Appendix A — Wait-Time Quick Reference

- **Primary care, urgent (clinical):** same-day / next-day.
- **Primary care, routine established:** within **10 business days**.
- **New patient (establish care):** within **15 business days**.
- **Preventive/wellness:** within **20 business days**.
- **Post-hospitalization follow-up:** within **7 calendar days**.
- **Specialty, urgent referral:** within **5 business days**.
- **Specialty, routine referral:** within **4 weeks**.
- **Telehealth:** same standard as the equivalent in-person visit type.
- Measured by **third-next-available**.

### 32.2 Appendix B — Cancellation / No-Show Quick Reference

- **Cancel** at least **24 hours** ahead (**48 hours** for longer/procedure visits) — no penalty.
- **Late cancellation:** inside the window; recorded; handled like a no-show for outreach.
- **No-show:** missed without advance cancellation; recorded; **barrier-first outreach** to rebook.
- **Repeated no-shows:** additional outreach, barrier resolution, care-team involvement; any consequence is a fair, noticed process applied only after barrier resolution and **never to urgent/emergency care**.

### 32.3 Appendix C — Referral Loop States

1. **Placed** (created, authorized if needed) → 2. **Scheduled** (within urgency timeframe) → 3. **Seen** (or no-show outreach) → 4. **Result returned** (to referring clinician) → 5. **Closed** (follow-up arranged). Owner: Referral Management Office, 585-230-2650.

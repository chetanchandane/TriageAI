## Part 1 of 7 — Purpose, Scope, Governance, Definitions, Contacts, and Financial-Care Philosophy

## 1. About This Policy

### 1.1 Title and Identification

This is the **Billing, Insurance, Financial Assistance & Medical Records Policy** of Lakeside Regional Medical Center (LRMC), policy document **FIN-700**. It governs how LRMC bills patients, handles insurance and coverage questions, offers and processes financial assistance, releases medical records in a HIPAA-compliant manner, and resolves billing disputes. It also defines how staff respond to the billing, insurance, financial-assistance, and records messages patients send through the MyLRMC portal.

LRMC is a 400-bed community teaching hospital and outpatient network at 1200 Lakeshore Parkway, Rochester, NY 14600. The hospital main line is 585-230-0000. **Patient Financial Services (billing) is reachable at 585-230-7000**, with direct sub-unit lines in the **585-230-3XXX** range listed in Section 5.1. **Health Information Management (Medical Records) is 585-230-4200**, with the Release of Information line at 585-230-3600.

### 1.2 Why This Policy Exists

Billing, insurance, financial assistance, and records are where many patients experience the health system as confusing or stressful. Clear, fair, and respectful handling of these matters is part of patient-centered care. This policy exists to:

1. **Make bills clear and fair.** Provide understandable statements, flexible payment options, and accurate billing, and correct errors promptly.
2. **Help patients afford care.** Offer financial assistance and counseling so that cost is not an insurmountable barrier, and so eligible patients receive the help they qualify for.
3. **Protect and provide records.** Release medical records to patients and authorized parties promptly and in compliance with HIPAA and state law, while safeguarding privacy.
4. **Resolve disputes fairly.** Give patients a clear, accessible process to question or dispute a bill and have it reviewed.

### 1.3 How to Use This Policy

Each numbered section stands on its own. If you need only the financial-assistance eligibility basics, the steps to request records, or the dispute procedure, you should be able to act from that single section. Timeframes here (for example, "itemized bills are provided within 10 business days of request") are **policy standards**; departures are documented with the reason.

### 1.4 A Boundary Note — Billing Is Not Clinical Triage

This policy governs **financial and records matters**, not clinical care. When a billing or records message also contains clinical content — a symptom, a medication concern, a health question — the clinical content is handled first under **PCT-100** and **EUA-150** (a possible emergency is directed to 911/ED), and the financial/records request is handled separately. Billing and records staff do not give medical advice.

### 1.5 Relationship to Other LRMC Policies

- **PCT-100 — Patient Communication and Triage Policy** (message triage; clinical content first).
- **PRIV-400 — Patient Privacy and Confidential Communications / Patient Rights, Privacy, Telehealth & Portal Use (PRIV-400)** — governs HIPAA, identity verification, proxy access, and the portal; records release here follows it.
- **HIM-300 — Documentation and the Legal Medical Record** — defines the record that is released under Part 5.

## 2. Scope and Applicability

### 2.1 What This Policy Covers

This policy applies to outpatient and hospital **patient billing, insurance and coverage support, financial assistance, medical-records release, and billing-dispute resolution** across LRMC and its affiliated network, including statements and payment options, insurance claims and coverage questions a patient raises, financial-assistance eligibility and application, HIPAA-compliant records release to patients and authorized third parties, itemized bills, and the dispute process.

### 2.2 What This Policy Does Not Cover

This policy does **not** govern: clinical care or triage (PCT-100, EUA-150); the internal coding and claims-submission operations beyond what patients interact with; insurer internal policies (LRMC assists patients but does not control their insurer); the detailed mechanics of the legal medical record (HIM-300); or the privacy and portal rules in full, which live in PRIV-400 (this policy applies them to records release).

### 2.3 Who Must Follow This Policy

This policy applies to Patient Financial Services and billing staff; financial counselors and financial-assistance staff; Health Information Management / Release of Information staff; patient-access and registration staff who collect coverage information; and any staff responding to billing, insurance, financial-assistance, or records messages. Only trained staff handle these requests, and identity/proxy verification under PRIV-400 is required before sharing financial or health information.

## 3. Financial-Care Philosophy

### 3.1 Care First, Billing Second

LRMC provides emergency and medically necessary care regardless of a patient's ability to pay; billing follows care and never blocks emergency treatment. No patient is denied emergency or urgent care because of an unpaid balance or insurance status.

### 3.2 Clarity and Respect

Patients receive understandable bills and respectful, judgment-free help with cost and coverage. Staff explain charges in plain language, offer payment options proactively, and treat financial difficulty as a problem to solve together, not a source of shame.

### 3.3 Proactive Financial Help

Financial assistance and counseling are offered proactively — at registration, on statements, after a large bill, and when a patient expresses concern about cost — not only when a patient knows to ask. Eligible patients are helped to apply.

### 3.4 Privacy by Default

Financial and health information is protected under PRIV-400 and HIPAA. Staff verify they are speaking with the patient or an authorized party before sharing account or record information, and disclose only the minimum necessary.

### 3.5 Fair, Reviewable Decisions

Billing and financial-assistance decisions are explained and reviewable. Patients can question a charge, request an itemized bill, and dispute a bill through a defined process (Part 6), and financial-assistance determinations include a path to provide more information or appeal.

## 5. Definitions and Contacts

**Statement / bill.** A summary of charges for services, what insurance paid or is expected to pay, and the patient's responsibility (the amount the patient owes).

**Explanation of Benefits (EOB).** A document from the patient's insurer (not a bill) explaining what the insurer covered and the patient's expected responsibility. Patients are taught to compare the EOB to the LRMC statement.

**Patient responsibility.** The portion the patient owes — for example, deductible, copay, coinsurance, or non-covered charges — after insurance.

**Deductible / copay / coinsurance.** Common cost-sharing terms: a deductible is what a patient pays before insurance contributes; a copay is a fixed amount for a service; coinsurance is a percentage of the cost the patient pays.

**Prior authorization.** Insurer approval required before certain services or medications are covered (coordinated with SRA-250 and RX-500).

**Financial assistance / charity care.** A program that reduces or eliminates the patient-responsibility portion for eligible patients based on income and circumstances (Part 4).

**Itemized bill.** A detailed, line-by-line list of every charge on an account (Part 6).

**Medical records release / Release of Information (ROI).** The HIPAA-compliant process of providing copies of a patient's medical record to the patient or an authorized party (Part 5).

**Authorization (records).** A signed, HIPAA-compliant permission allowing release of a patient's records to a specified party for a specified purpose (Part 5; PRIV-400).

**Proxy / authorized representative.** A person authorized to act on a patient's behalf for billing and/or records under PRIV-400 (for example, a parent, guardian, or holder of appropriate authority).

## Part 2 of 7 — Billing Statements and Payment Options

## 6. Understanding the Statement

### 6.1 What the Statement Shows

An LRMC statement shows the services provided, the total charges, the amount billed to and paid by insurance (where applicable), any adjustments, and the **patient responsibility** — the amount the patient owes. Statements are written to be understandable, with a summary of what is owed and why, and contact information for questions (Billing Customer Service, 585-230-3100).

### 6.2 Statement Timing and the EOB

Patients with insurance typically receive an **Explanation of Benefits (EOB)** from their insurer first; the EOB is **not** a bill. LRMC's statement follows once the claim is processed. Staff encourage patients to compare the EOB and the statement and to call Billing Customer Service (585-230-3100) if the patient responsibility differs from what they expected.

### 6.3 Accessing Statements

Patients can view and pay statements through the **MyLRMC** portal (online bill pay), receive paper statements by mail, or request statements by phone (585-230-3100). Portal help is available from the MyLRMC Help Desk (585-230-3000).

## 7. Payment Options

### 7.1 Ways to Pay

LRMC accepts payment through the MyLRMC portal, by mail, by phone (585-230-3100), and in person at the cashier. Patients can pay in full or set up a plan (Section 7.2). Staff never pressure a patient to pay an amount they cannot afford and instead offer payment plans, counseling, and financial assistance.

### 7.2 Payment Plans

Patients who cannot pay a balance in full may set up an **interest-free payment plan** with affordable monthly amounts based on the balance and the patient's circumstances. Plans are arranged through Billing Customer Service (585-230-3100) or financial counseling (585-230-3400). The terms are explained clearly, and patients can adjust a plan if their circumstances change.

### 7.3 Price Estimates and Financial Counseling

Before scheduled services, patients may request a **good-faith price estimate** and speak with a financial counselor (Financial Counseling & Price Estimates, 585-230-3400) about expected costs, coverage, and assistance options. Estimates help patients plan and avoid surprises; counselors also screen for financial-assistance eligibility (Part 4).

### 7.4 If a Patient Cannot Pay

A patient who cannot pay is offered, in order: a review of insurance and coverage (Part 3), a payment plan (Section 7.2), and screening for financial assistance (Part 4). LRMC works with patients to find a workable path and does not move an account toward collections without offering these options and reasonable notice. Emergency and medically necessary care is never withheld for inability to pay.

## Part 3 of 7 — Insurance and Coverage Questions

## 8. How LRMC Helps With Insurance

### 8.1 What Staff Can and Cannot Do

LRMC's Insurance & Coverage team (585-230-3200) helps patients understand their LRMC charges, submits claims to the patient's insurer, and assists with coverage questions and appeals related to LRMC services. Staff **cannot** change the insurer's rules or guarantee what a plan will cover — coverage decisions belong to the insurer — but they advocate for the patient and explain options.

### 8.2 Verifying Coverage

At registration and before scheduled services, patient-access staff verify the patient's insurance and flag any need for referral or prior authorization (coordinated with SRA-250 and RX-500). Patients are encouraged to keep their insurance information current in MyLRMC and to notify LRMC of changes.

### 8.3 Common Coverage Questions

- **"Why do I owe this?"** Often the amount reflects a deductible, copay, coinsurance, or a non-covered service per the patient's plan. Staff explain the patient-responsibility breakdown and compare it to the EOB.
- **"Was this covered?"** Staff review how the claim was processed and whether it can be resubmitted or appealed if there was an error.
- **"Do I need prior authorization?"** Some services and medications require insurer approval in advance; staff help obtain it (SRA-250, RX-500).
- **"I have new insurance."** Staff update coverage and, where appropriate, rebill the correct insurer.

### 8.4 Claims, Denials, and Appeals

When an insurer denies or underpays a claim for an LRMC service, staff review the reason and, where appropriate, correct and resubmit the claim or assist the patient with the insurer's appeal process. Patients are kept informed, and any patient-responsibility amount is not aggressively pursued while a legitimate claim issue or appeal is being resolved.

### 8.5 Uninsured Patients

Uninsured patients are offered help applying for coverage they may qualify for, self-pay discounts where available, price estimates and counseling (585-230-3400), and financial assistance (Part 4). No patient is turned away from emergency or medically necessary care for lack of insurance.

## Part 4 of 7 — Financial Assistance: Eligibility and Application

## 9. The Financial Assistance Program

### 9.1 Purpose

LRMC's **Financial Assistance Program** (also called charity care) reduces or eliminates the patient-responsibility portion of bills for eligible patients who cannot afford medically necessary care. The program exists so that cost is not a barrier to needed care. The Financial Assistance & Charity Care Office is reachable at **585-230-3300**.

### 9.2 Who May Be Eligible

Eligibility is based primarily on **household income relative to the federal poverty guidelines** and the patient's financial circumstances, and may also consider assets, family size, and extraordinary medical expenses. The program generally serves uninsured and underinsured patients, and patients facing financial hardship from medical bills. Specific income thresholds and discount tiers are published in the program's plain-language summary and are reviewed at least annually. Patients are encouraged to apply even if unsure, because partial assistance may be available across a range of incomes.

### 9.3 Services Covered

Financial assistance applies to emergency and medically necessary care provided by LRMC. It generally does not cover purely elective or cosmetic services not deemed medically necessary. The program summary lists what is included.

## 10. Applying for Financial Assistance

### 10.1 How to Apply

Patients apply by completing the **Financial Assistance Application**, available through the MyLRMC portal, by mail, or from the Financial Assistance Office (585-230-3300) and financial counselors (585-230-3400). Staff help patients complete the application and gather documents, and interpretation is available (585-230-6400).

### 10.2 Documentation

The application typically requests household size, income information (such as recent pay stubs, a tax return, or a benefits statement), and basic financial details. Staff request only what is necessary and help patients who have difficulty providing documents. Incomplete applications are not simply denied — staff reach out to help complete them.

### 10.3 Timeline and Determination

LRMC reviews a complete application and provides a written **determination generally within 30 days**, stating whether assistance is approved, the discount applied, and the patient responsibility (if any). While an application is pending, eligible accounts are held and not sent to collections.

### 10.4 Approval, Denial, and Appeal

- **Approved:** the discount is applied to eligible balances, and the patient is told the remaining responsibility, if any, and any payment-plan option.
- **More information needed:** staff contact the patient to complete the application rather than denying it outright.
- **Denied:** the patient is told the reason and how to **appeal or reapply**, including providing additional information or a change in circumstances. Financial counselors (585-230-3400) assist with appeals.

### 10.5 Retroactive and Presumptive Assistance

A patient may apply for assistance on recent bills even after receiving them, within the program's lookback period, and qualifying amounts are adjusted retroactively. Where allowed, LRMC may grant **presumptive eligibility** based on available information for patients who clearly qualify, without a full application.

### 10.6 Protections During the Process

Patients applying for or receiving financial assistance are treated with confidentiality and respect. Accounts under review are not advanced to collections, and patients are connected to additional resources (such as help applying for public coverage) as appropriate.

## Part 5 of 7 — Medical Records Release (HIPAA-Compliant) Process

## 11. The Patient's Right to Their Records

### 11.1 Right of Access

Under HIPAA, patients have the right to access and obtain a copy of their own medical records held by LRMC, with limited exceptions defined by law. LRMC honors this right promptly through the **Release of Information (ROI)** process. The ROI line is **585-230-3600**, and Health Information Management is reachable at 585-230-4200. Records release is governed by HIPAA, state law, PRIV-400, and HIM-300.

### 11.2 What Patients Can Request

Patients may request copies of records such as visit notes, test and laboratory results, imaging reports, medication lists, and other parts of their legal medical record. Patients may also request that records be **sent to a third party** they designate (for example, another provider) with a proper authorization.

## 12. How to Request Records

### 12.1 Submitting a Request

Records requests are made through the **MyLRMC** portal, by submitting a completed **records-request/authorization form** to Release of Information (585-230-3600), or in person. The request specifies the patient, the records or date range sought, the format (electronic or paper), and where to send them. Staff help patients complete the form, with interpretation available (585-230-6400).

### 12.2 Identity and Authorization

Before releasing records, staff verify the requester's identity and authority under PRIV-400:

- **The patient** requesting their own records provides identity verification.
- **A proxy or authorized representative** must have verified authority (for example, a parent of a minor, a guardian, or a personal representative) per PRIV-400.
- **A third party** (such as another provider, an attorney, or an employer) must provide a **signed, HIPAA-compliant authorization** from the patient specifying what may be released, to whom, and for what purpose. Authorizations can be revoked by the patient.

### 12.3 Sensitive Information

Certain categories — such as mental-health, substance-use, HIV, and reproductive-health information — have additional legal protections and may require specific authorization. Staff apply these protections and release sensitive information only with the appropriate, specific authorization (PRIV-400).

### 12.4 Timeframe

LRMC provides requested records **generally within 10 business days** of receiving a complete, valid request (and within the maximum period required by law), and sooner when feasible, especially for continuity-of-care needs. If more time is required, the patient is notified of the reason and the expected date.

### 12.5 Fees

Patients may obtain their own records at no cost or for a reasonable, cost-based fee where permitted by law; many electronic copies through the portal are provided free. Any allowable fee is disclosed in advance. Fees are never used to obstruct a patient's access to their records.

### 12.6 Records for Continuity of Care

When records are needed to support ongoing care (for example, a referral to a specialist under SRA-250), LRMC facilitates timely transfer between treating providers consistent with HIPAA's treatment provisions, without unnecessary delay.

### 12.7 Amendments and Accounting of Disclosures

Patients may request a **correction (amendment)** to their record if they believe something is inaccurate, and may request an **accounting of certain disclosures** of their information, under PRIV-400 and HIM-300. Staff explain how to make these requests and the timeframe for a response.

## Part 6 of 7 — Itemized Bills and Billing Dispute Procedures

## 13. Itemized Bills

### 13.1 Right to an Itemized Bill

Any patient may request a **fully itemized bill** — a line-by-line list of every charge on their account, with descriptions — at no cost. Itemized bills help patients understand and verify charges. Requests are made to Billing Dispute & Itemized-Bill Requests (585-230-3700) or Billing Customer Service (585-230-3100).

### 13.2 Timeframe

LRMC provides an itemized bill **within 10 business days** of request. Staff offer to walk the patient through the line items and explain any unfamiliar terms or charges.

## 14. Billing Disputes

### 14.1 Raising a Dispute

A patient who believes a bill is incorrect — a charge for a service not received, a duplicate charge, an incorrect insurance application, or an unexpected amount — may dispute it through Billing Dispute (585-230-3700), the MyLRMC portal, by phone, or in writing. Staff treat disputes as legitimate questions to investigate, not as adversarial.

### 14.2 The Dispute Process

1. **Acknowledge** the dispute promptly and explain the process and expected timeline.
2. **Hold collection activity** on the disputed amount while it is reviewed, so the patient is not pursued for a contested charge.
3. **Investigate** — review the account, the itemized charges, the medical record where relevant, and the insurance processing; correct genuine errors.
4. **Respond in writing** with the findings — the charge is correct (with an explanation), corrected/adjusted, or resubmitted to insurance — generally **within 30 days**.
5. **Offer next steps** — if the patient remains dissatisfied, explain how to escalate, including review by a supervisor and involvement of the Patient Advocate (585-230-4000).

### 14.3 Corrections and Refunds

When a dispute finds an error in the patient's favor, LRMC corrects the account and issues any **refund** owed promptly. When an insurer error is found, staff resubmit or appeal the claim. The patient is told the outcome and any remaining responsibility.

### 14.4 Protection From Aggressive Collection

LRMC follows fair billing and collection practices: patients receive clear statements and reasonable notice before any collection step; accounts under active dispute, financial-assistance review, or insurance appeal are not advanced to collections; and emergency/medically necessary care is never withheld due to a balance. Patients facing collection who need help are connected to financial counseling (585-230-3400) and the Patient Advocate (585-230-4000).

## Part 7 of 7 — Responding to Billing and Records Messages, FAQ, and Appendices

## 15. How to Respond to These Messages

Every reply to a billing, insurance, financial-assistance, or records message follows these rules, restated here because this section may be read alone:

1. **Triage clinical content first.** If the message contains a symptom or health concern, handle it under PCT-100/EUA-150 first (911/ED for an emergency); then address the financial/records request.
2. **Verify identity/authority** under PRIV-400 before sharing account or record details.
3. **Be clear and respectful.** Explain charges and options in plain language; offer payment plans, counseling, and assistance proactively.
4. **Protect privacy.** Share only the minimum necessary; apply extra protection to sensitive records.
5. **Give a next step, owner, and timeframe**, and the right direct contact.
6. **Document** per HIM-300.

## 16. Common Patient Questions

### 16.1 "When will I get my bill, and is the EOB a bill?"

If you have insurance, you'll usually get an Explanation of Benefits (EOB) from your insurer first — that's **not** a bill, just a summary of what they covered. Our statement comes after your claim is processed and shows what you owe. Compare the two, and if the amount looks off, call Billing Customer Service at 585-230-3100.

### 16.2 "Can I set up a payment plan?"

Yes. We offer interest-free payment plans with monthly amounts based on your balance and situation. Set one up through Billing Customer Service (585-230-3100) or Financial Counseling (585-230-3400). You can adjust the plan if your circumstances change, and we'd rather set up an affordable plan than send your account to collections.

### 16.3 "Do I qualify for financial assistance?"

You might — our program is based mainly on household income and circumstances, and partial help is available across a range of incomes, so it's worth applying even if you're unsure. Contact Financial Assistance at 585-230-3300, and we'll help you apply and hold your eligible accounts while we review. Decisions generally come within 30 days.

### 16.4 "How do I get my medical records, and is there a fee?"

Request them through MyLRMC or Release of Information at 585-230-3600. We'll verify your identity and provide them generally within 10 business days. Many electronic copies through the portal are free; any allowable fee for other formats is small and disclosed in advance. We can also send records to another provider with your signed authorization.

### 16.5 "Can someone else handle my bills or get my records?"

Yes, if they're set up as your authorized representative or proxy under our privacy rules, or — for records to a third party — with your signed, HIPAA-compliant authorization. We verify authority before sharing any financial or health information. To set up a proxy, contact the MyLRMC Help Desk at 585-230-3000.

### 16.6 "What if I can't resolve a billing problem?"

If you've worked with our billing team and still aren't satisfied, you can escalate to a supervisor and to our Patient Advocate at 585-230-4000, who helps resolve concerns. We'll keep the disputed amount on hold while it's being reviewed, and we'll explain every step.
